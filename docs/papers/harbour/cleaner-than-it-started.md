# Can a task finish with the codebase cleaner than it started?

**Areas read better than the pair baseline; the Periodicals agree on direction; tasks on average: not shown.** Nine area readings over fifteen tickets read better in both blind passes, and no pass read one worse. The pair baseline is four ordinary pairs of consecutive changes to the same files, from one day in October, picked by a rule fixed before the results and read with the same prompt. One of the four reads better in both passes. So reading a pair as one area does not by itself make it read better; this is not loop against no loop. The one better pair was a consolidation, so the supported claim is narrower: consolidation reads better, and the loop's finished moves in this record are of that kind. Drift & Coherence run 5 reads `af55cb73..5c63aa26` as better, small, with one area mixed; Seams & Tangles run 9 reads `af55cb73..90542b2c` with no area worse, and run 8's worse area stands. The record does not show whether a task on average finishes cleaner. The limits matter most: the blind prompt is partly circular, the baseline is four pairs in one repository, and seven merges after both Periodical windows have only the blind reading.

**Terms.** *Harbour* is the product whose codebase this is; LinearViewer is its repository, and simple-dispatcher is the second repository the loop touched. A *ticket* is a Linear issue; a *merge* is a first-parent commit on `main`. The *place* is what a later change has to work within: its owners, copies, lists and guards. A *place row* is a merge that a loop ticket landed as part of its work on the place; the place ledger (`docs/plans/survey/place-ledger.md`) keeps one row per such merge. A *tidy-first* is a small tidy filed and landed ahead of a broad ticket; a *tidy-after* is the follow-on that removes what a parent left behind. A *blind pass* is a reader that scores a commit from its code only, without the ticket, and writes a sign (better, neutral, mixed or worse); its sign is the *Balance* cell of that reader's table. The blind passes are labelled batches A to D (four comments, each with 14 rows), single or area passes (one change or one area each), and the pair-baseline passes AW to BD. A *Periodical* is a periodic review report in `docs/reviews/` (Seams & Tangles, Drift & Coherence, Comprehension-Debt), which reads the codebase directly. *Census* is the list of hand-written fake DOMs in the test suite. A *Survey* is the read-only step before a ticket's work, and an *after-answer* is the builder's reply after the work. The *scope call* is the "How to go about it" section of a ticket. The *operator* is the person who ran the Survey loop and kept its notebook, with Claude. The *loop* is the sequence run on each ticket: a read-only Survey before the work, a scope call (written by the person, or by Claude on the person's delegation), the build, and a place reading with the builder's after-answer at merge. An *area* is a parent with its tidy-after or its pieces, read as one unit; LIN-4254 read alone is an area. A *pass* is one blind reader's scoring of one batch or one change; pass 1 and pass 2 are the two sweeps over batches A to D. The *baseline* is the place rows in the ledger's baseline window (`015aa4c6..5334c81a`): nine rows, four from 3 November and five from 4 November, one of the five being the simple-dispatcher half of LIN-4202. They are compared with the loop's forward rows and are not loop changes. The *pair baseline* is a different thing, and this version's name for it: four ordinary pairs of consecutive changes to the same files, from 20 October, picked by a rule fixed before any result, and read as areas with the same prompt. Its one claim is that reading a pair as one area does not by itself make it read better.

## Method

The population is every first-parent merge from 4 Nov 00:00 UTC to the two pins: 76 in LinearViewer and 20 in simple-dispatcher, 96 in all. Of those 96, 67 are place merges: 62 loop merges (51 in LinearViewer, 11 in simple-dispatcher) and five baseline place merges from 4 November (LIN-4178, 4179, 4198, and LIN-4202 in both repositories). Nine are merges the ledger marks as outside it, 19 are Periodical, paper, notebook or scripts merges, and one is a parent issue. The 104 rows add to those eight 3 November rows: four baseline place merges (LIN-4166, 4168, 4173 and 4177) and four UI context rows. The 71 place rows on 68 tickets are therefore the 62 loop merges plus the nine baseline rows. The population is frozen by `freeze.mjs`, which reads LIN-4150 first and asserts that batches A to D, and all 62 blind passes, are inside it. The cutoff in the inputs is 12:02:19.388Z, the posting time of the last pass the paper uses; no comment on LIN-4150 was posted after it. Then the freeze reads each ticket's issue, cost (per dispatch) and dispatch history, and the 74 custom prompts dispatched since 4 Nov, the after-answers among them. Every answer is saved raw with its read time. The pair-baseline tickets are read for their issue only, because the pair claim needs only the merges and the passes. The freeze is GET-only.

The blind reads are the primary instrument. Each pass scores a commit, or a ticket or area, from the code only, in one of three forms: batches of 14 single commits, where batches A and B make pass 1 over 28 commits and C and D make pass 2 over the same 28 in a different order, except that pass 1's batch B row 12 (LIN-4240's LinearViewer commit) was scored together with LIN-4239's simple-dispatcher commit in B3 as one cross-repo unit, as the prompt instructs, so pass 1 has 27 signed units against pass 2's 28 (pass 2 scores them separately, D1 and D10) (`blind-codes.json`); one change per session, or one area per session; and the eight pair-baseline passes AW to BD, two passes on each of four pairs. The sign is the Balance cell of the pass's own table, copied verbatim, and mapped to +1 for better, 0 for neutral or mixed, and -1 for worse. `code-blind.mjs` does the copying, so no sign was re-judged here. The operator's running tally of 17 better, 3 mixed, 1 neutral and 0 worse, kept in the notebook, was overturned by the blind reads, and the ledger says so (`docs/plans/survey/place-ledger.md@1d10f9b3:127`). The Survey status line in `docs/plans/survey.md@1d10f9b3:5` is replaced here by a pointer.

The Periodicals are the instruments that do not share the blind prompt. Seams & Tangles run 8, Drift & Coherence run 4 and Comprehension-Debt run 5 each ground LinearViewer at `af55cb73`. Seams run 9 grounds at `90542b2c`, and Drift run 5 at `5c63aa26`. Drift run 3 grounds simple-dispatcher at `07c88f7` and reads `d8d186c..07c88f7` (`docs/reviews/drift-coherence-review-2026-11-05.md@1d10f9b3:9,17`). That corrects the research comment, which said Comprehension-Debt was the only reader of those commits. The census is read with `git show` at each merge. Costs are the frozen per-dispatch reads. The paper's numbers come from `analysis.json`, which reads the frozen folder alone and reproduces byte for byte. The few counts and ratios that are arithmetic on its keys are shown in the text: 62 is the number of loop place rows (71 place rows less the nine baseline rows), and 65% is $7.82 over $11.94.

The Survey recount and the pair rule are each run on the frozen folder. The pair rule is the one written in the log before any result, and it picks the four pairs that the log picked (`pair_baseline.selection.pairs.matches_expected`). If it had picked any other four, the analysis would have stopped and reported the difference.

To reproduce, from the LinearViewer root, run the four scripts in this order, each with `--inputs` (the invocation in the header of `inputs.mjs`; version 1's README predates the flag). The freeze needs `HARBOUR_LOCAL_BASE` and reads the workspace API; the other three read the folder alone, and `census-by-merge` rewrites `manifest.json`'s read time, so run it on a copy when you need to compare hashes:

## Limits

- **Small numbers.** The baseline is eight changes in the blind passes. The forward rows are nineteen or twenty. The area readings are nine, over fifteen tickets. The pair baseline is four pairs. The cost pairs are three. The Finding 5 pattern rests on eleven cases, six that deleted copies and five that added code beside them, and the cases are the ones the blind prompt's question about copies picks out.
- **The blind reads are partly circular.** The blind prompt is frozen in the evidence folder (`prompts/blind-read-prompt.md`), copied verbatim from the operator's answer on LIN-4315. The circularity remains, and it applies to the pair baseline too: the same prompt scored the pairs, and it asks every change whether it added a copy and what it deleted. The tidy-after is the change that answers that question, and the same prompt judges its result. The Periodicals answer this for area direction, because they read the same areas without the prompt, but only through `af55cb73`, and Seams run 9 and Drift run 5 agree with the blind readings on the windows they cover. They do not answer it for any single change.
- **The baseline is not an untouched area.** The pair baseline is four ordinary pre-loop pairs, read as areas, in one repository, from one day (20 October, 00:41Z to 13:27Z), with one prompt. Its pairs are not untouched areas, and it does not compare loop with no loop. It narrows the earlier limit, "no like-for-like baseline for areas"; it does not lift it. Version 1's proposal line for an untouched area is still open (see Next 2).
- **"Covered" in the evidence table is loose.** The `periodical_reads` column of `evidence-table.tsv` says "covered" for a LinearViewer row inside at least one Periodical window. For the rows before that window (the 3 November rows, the earlier 4 November rows, and `d7ceb373` itself) that is, among the Periodicals cited here, Comprehension-Debt run 5 alone, and the frozen column does not say so.
- **Seven merges after both Periodical windows.** The windows end at `90542b2c` (Seams run 9) and `5c63aa26` (Drift run 5). The merges after both are LIN-4312, LIN-4322 and LIN-4254's `afe9074f` among the place merges, and LIN-4323, 4324, 4332 and 4333 among the others. Only the blind prompt reads the three place merges. Drift run 5's own caveats (one area mixed, "production code shaped for tests") are stated beside the census, not folded into it.
- **The new readings are unchecked by a reader who did not write them.** The readings of LIN-4313+4317, LIN-4296+4254 and LIN-4254 were made by the same prompt as the rest. Two fresh readers, one for accuracy and one for clarity, are planned before merge and have not run yet; their changes will be recorded in the `model:` line.
- **Cost is live and partial.** Three place rows are partial. A parent's cost excludes its children, so LIN-4255's $56.10 and its pieces' $95.03 are separate figures.
- **Where the notebook and the code disagree, the code wins.** The notebook's 17 / 3 / 1 / 0 is overturned, and this paper replaces it. The ledger's "+0.37 against +0.38" compares across passes. The ledger's cost for LIN-4277 is stale. The census is 6 at `509f797e`, all `// stop:`, and the ticket's Surveys carried 22, the count after LIN-4296, not the pin's. The research comment listed LIN-4178 and LIN-4181 as partial; the frozen reads show LIN-4173, 4177 and 4178, and 4181 is outside the ledger. The notebook's clock headings drift from git and comment times by up to about 1.5 hours on 6 November, so this paper cites git and comment times throughout.
- **The Periodicals are instruments, not an audit.** They are written by the same kind of agent that wrote the code. They agree with each other on direction, and they disagree on one area. Seams run 9 is also a delta report: it reads only what changed since run 8.
- **The Surveys are visible only on the record the proxy returns.** Version 2's freeze reads all 143 comments on LIN-4150, so the Surveys before 5 November are now in reach. The Survey count depends on the rule written before the freeze, and the stricter rule is not the version 1 rule (Finding 10).
