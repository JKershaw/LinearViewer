# Can a task finish with the codebase cleaner than it started?

**Tasks on average: not shown. Areas and finished moves: yes, on few cases.** Over 4 to 6 November the loop ran on 58 place merges (and four baseline merges from 3 November), 59 place tickets in all, across LinearViewer and simple-dispatcher. On the one like-for-like blind read, the ordinary tickets came out level with the baseline or behind it, and two of them (LIN-4241 and LIN-4212) read worse in at least one pass. They did not visibly make the codebase better. Where a move was finished, or a tidy-after cleaned up after its parent, the area read better in both blind passes: six area readings covering eleven tickets, every pass better and none worse. The independent Periodicals read the window as better, but small. Two of them, Seams & Tangles run 8 and Comprehension-Debt run 5, each name an area that got worse. The net direction, if there is one, rests on the area readings and the Periodicals, not on the ordinary tickets. Two limits matter most. The blind area reads score prompts that name the copies a tidy-after then removes, so they are partly circular. And the independent Periodicals read only to `af55cb73`, so the five merges after it have no independent reading. The Limits section lists the rest.

**Terms.** A *ticket* is a Linear issue; a *merge* is a first-parent commit on `main`. A *place row* is a merge that changed the codebase's place as part of a loop ticket; the place ledger (`docs/plans/survey/place-ledger.md`) keeps one row per such merge. A *tidy-first* is a small tidy filed and landed ahead of a broad ticket; a *tidy-after* is the follow-on that removes what a parent left behind. A *blind pass* is a reader that scores a commit from its code only, without the ticket, and writes a sign (better, neutral, mixed or worse); its sign is the *Balance* cell of that reader's table. The blind passes are labelled batches A to D (four comments, each with 14 rows) and E to AP (one change or one area each). A *Periodical* is a periodic review report in `docs/reviews/` (Seams & Tangles, Drift & Coherence, Comprehension-Debt), which reads the codebase directly. *Census* is the list of hand-written fake DOMs in the test suite. A *Survey* is the read-only step before a ticket's work, and an *after-answer* is the builder's reply after the work. The *scope call* is the "How to go about it" section of a ticket. The *operator* is the person who ran the Survey loop and kept its notebook, with Claude.

## Findings

**1. Ordinary tickets stayed level with the baseline.** In the two blind passes the eight baseline rows (the ledger's own baseline window, `015aa4c6..5334c81a`) scored +0.38 in pass 1 and +0.50 in pass 2. The forward rows the ledger calls surveyed, excluding LIN-4212, which it calls unsurveyed, scored +0.39 over 18 rows in pass 1 and +0.37 over 19 rows in pass 2. With LIN-4212 counted, pass 1 is +0.32 over 19 rows and pass 2 is +0.35 over 20. The ledger's "+0.37" for pass 1 does not reproduce from the frozen comments (`docs/plans/survey/place-ledger.md@1d10f9b3:106`). With n of eight and nineteen, a gap of a tenth is noise. Pass 2 has the baseline ahead of the ordinary rows (+0.50 against +0.37), and pass 1 has them level (+0.38 against +0.39). Two forward rows read worse: LIN-4241 in both passes, and LIN-4212 in pass 1 ("mixed, leans worse" in pass 2). The reading is: ordinary tickets did not lift areas on this instrument, two of them read worse, and in pass 2 the forward rows scored behind the baseline. Neither gap is one to lean on.

**2. Finished moves and tidy-afters read better in both passes, and no reading was worse.** Nineteen single or area subjects were each read in two passes. Twelve read better in both: six areas covering eleven tickets (LIN-4247+4246+4293, LIN-4251+4277, LIN-4252+4255, LIN-4255 alone, LIN-4263+4276, LIN-4292+4304) and six single tickets (LIN-4253, 4276, 4277, 4293, 4296, 4304). Two read better, then mixed (LIN-4251, LIN-4292). Five read mixed in both (LIN-4246, 4247, 4248, 4252, 4263). No pass wrote worse. The research comment on LIN-4315 counted five area reads and two single changes. The comments show one more area (LIN-4255 read alone, passes AA and AB) and four more single reads (the tidy-afters).

**3. The independent readers agree on direction, and two of them name an area that got worse.** Drift & Coherence run 4 rates the window "better, small" overall (`docs/reviews/drift-coherence-review-2026-11-05-run4.md@1d10f9b3:29`). Seams & Tangles run 8 rates "other features: worse as a map": the catch-all grew from 16 to 17 files because of one new leaf file, `lib/stage-writes.js`, which LIN-4253 added (PR #2290). LIN-4253 is a tidy-first, and both its blind passes read better. Those passes read the new file as having "an obvious home" (pass H). Comprehension-Debt run 5 rates 12 of 21 areas better for a cold reader, six unchanged, one mixed and two worse: `lib/task-page-loader.js`, which assembles Home's run offers at the pin, and the rationale pointers that deleted files left behind (`docs/reviews/comprehension-debt-review-2026-11-05.md@1d10f9b3:158,164`). So the summary in the research comment, that no area the loop touched read worse, does not hold. The Seams reading and the Comprehension reading each name a worse area, and the blind reads on the LIN-4253 commit did not catch the catch-all.

**4. One tidy-first finished its own move, one stopped part way, and one control read mixed.** LIN-4253 deleted two hand-kept name lists and derived them from each template, and read better in both passes. LIN-4252 added a shared fake DOM helper with 17 hand-written copies still outstanding (pass I), and read mixed in both. LIN-4247, which the ticket set as the control, read mixed in both. Each is one case. What they support is that the finish matters, not that a tidy-first does.

**5. The mechanism the readings name: a new owner that leaves its copies running reads mixed, and deleting the copies reads better.** Six single readings that read better in both passes each deleted or merged copies: LIN-4253 (two name lists), 4276 (a private comparator), 4277 (a reworded second copy of the endpoint hints), 4293 (`state.go` literals replaced by one constructor), 4296 (`<select>` selectedness moved to the shared fake) and 4304 (19 method-presence guards removed). Five readings that added code beside the old copies read mixed in both passes: LIN-4246 (the new vm layer, with 16 suites still building their own sandbox), 4247 (a new `runEnded` owner, with the object literals it sits beside still copied), 4248 (two templates that repeat a closing paragraph), 4252 (the helper, with 17 copies left) and 4263 (a per-dispatch split whose rule is written out in five places, per the LIN-4263+4276 pass). The batch comments name the same pattern in their summaries: consolidations come in two steps, and a list one commit creates is often not updated by the next. This is eleven cases, read by one prompt, and the circularity limit below applies to all of them.

**6. The census count recorded the fake-DOM move; it does not measure it.** The list of hand-written fake DOMs was 56 when it was introduced at LIN-4265 (5 Nov 13:18). It was 25 at `af55cb73`, 22 after LIN-4296, and 18 at `1d10f9b3`: 12 movable and 6 marked `// stop:`, the entries that need a real browser API the fake cannot model (`tests/unit/fake-dom-census.test.js@1d10f9b3:52-71`). LIN-4254, the move, is in flight. At the freeze its pieces 0 and 1 (LIN-4306, LIN-4307) were Done, piece 2 (LIN-4308) was In Progress, and pieces 3 to 6 were Todo. So 18 is where the count stands, not where the move finishes. The count moves when a file leaves the list, not when the copies behind it go. It is a proxy, and the blind reads, not the count, are what show the deletions.

**7. One ticket walked through the loop: LIN-4276.** LIN-4263's `/cost` per-dispatch split was read mixed in both passes (Q and R). LIN-4276, its tidy-after, is the next step of that loop. Its ticket carries the pre-work Surveys (open-0 and global), a written scope call ("How to go about it"), and an after-answer ("Survey 1a (round 3)"). It merged at 14:17 on 5 November, before `af55cb73`, so the Periodicals cover it. Its blind reads (passes S and T) found the private `rankOf` comparator gone: its `Infinity - Infinity` gave NaN for two undated rows, and it reuses `byDispatchOrder`. They found the `[id, _id, loopId]` posting-key list written once instead of twice. They also noted two comparators the change left alone, `lib/dispatch-lineage.js@1d10f9b3:162` and `lib/recent-runs.js@1d10f9b3:46`, and read the change better anyway. The paired read with LIN-4263 was better too (passes V and U). The cost was $6.77 against the parent's $14.37, 47%. This is the clearest case in the record. It is also a case where the blind prompt names the copies in advance, which is the circularity limit below.

**8. Cost is measured here, not optimised.** Of the 62 place rows, 59 have an exact cost and three are partial: LIN-4173, 4177 and 4178, which include an unpriced model, so their cost is a floor. The median exact place row cost $8.41 (n = 59). Each tidy-after cost a share of its parent: 47% for LIN-4276 against LIN-4263, 65% for LIN-4277 against LIN-4251 ($7.82 against $11.94, live; the ledger's $5.93 for LIN-4277 is out of date) and 85% for LIN-4304 against LIN-4292. That is three pairs, so a range, not a rate. The fake-DOM move cost $95.03 across its 14 pieces, all exact. LIN-4255, its parent, cost $56.10 on its own, and its pieces are not in that figure. LIN-4271, another parent of the same work, has no merge row of its own in the window; its own cost is $22.07 and is not included in the $95.03. The builders' after-answers are present on 30 place tickets. They were counted here and not scored.

**9. The person's role: the scope calls, where they were written, and where they were not.** The scope call ("How to go about it") is on 20 of 59 place tickets. It is absent on 39, including the two tidy-firsts LIN-4252 and LIN-4253 and the follow-on LIN-4246. A pre-work Survey is visible on the ticket or on LIN-4150 for 17 of 59. Of the 42 without one, 16 are pieces of the fake-DOM work (LIN-4255's 14 pieces and LIN-4254's first two, which carry no Survey of their own), four are 3 Nov baseline rows whose Surveys, if they had any, were posted before the cap, and 22 are 4 Nov rows whose Surveys the proxy cannot show, for the reason given under Limits. The ledger records one scope wording that did damage: LIN-4178's copy is "a new twin, invited by the ticket's own wording" (`docs/plans/survey/place-ledger.md@1d10f9b3:19`). The record therefore offers one case for one reading: the scope call is where copies get invited, and a tidy-first is where they are removed. That is a hypothesis for the Library to test, not a finding. The operator's account names two dials for each ticket, how much to tidy and whether to make no new mess. The frozen record does not show the dials directly. The scope call is the only place they would appear, and it is absent on two-thirds of the place tickets.

## Method

The population is every first-parent merge from 4 Nov 00:00 UTC to the two pins: 62 in LinearViewer and 20 in simple-dispatcher, 82 in all. Adding the baseline rows the ledger names (merged 3 and 4 Nov) and the UI context rows gives 90 rows. Of those, 62 are place rows on 59 tickets. The rest are nine pre-loop merges in both repositories that the ledger marks as not in the ledger, 14 Periodical, paper or notebook merges, one parent issue and four UI context rows. The population is frozen by `freeze.mjs`, which reads LIN-4150 first because the proxy returns at most 50 comments per issue. LIN-4150 returned exactly 50, and the freeze asserts that batches A to D, and all 42 blind passes, are inside them (`lib/providers/linear/index.js@1d10f9b3:1516`). Then it reads each ticket's issue, cost (per dispatch) and dispatch history, and the 73 custom prompts dispatched since 4 Nov, the after-answers among them. Every answer is saved raw with its read time.

The blind reads are the primary instrument. Each pass scores a commit, or a ticket or area, from the code only, in one of three forms: batches of 14 single commits, where batches A and B make pass 1 over 28 commits and C and D make pass 2 over the same 28; one change per session; and one parent with its tidy-after per session. The sign is the Balance cell of the pass's own table, copied verbatim, and mapped to +1 for better, 0 for neutral or mixed, and -1 for worse. `code-blind.mjs` does the copying, so no sign was re-judged here. The operator's running tally of 17 better, 3 mixed, 1 neutral and 0 worse, kept in the notebook, was overturned by the blind reads, and the ledger says so (`docs/plans/survey/place-ledger.md@1d10f9b3:127`). The Survey status line in `docs/plans/survey.md@1d10f9b3:5` is replaced here by a pointer.

The Periodicals are the independent instruments. Seams & Tangles run 8, Drift & Coherence run 4 and Comprehension-Debt run 5 each ground LinearViewer at `af55cb73`. Drift run 3 grounds simple-dispatcher at `07c88f7` and reads `d8d186c..07c88f7` (`docs/reviews/drift-coherence-review-2026-11-05.md@1d10f9b3:9,17`). That corrects the research comment, which said Comprehension-Debt was the only reader of those commits. The census is read with `git show` at each merge. Costs are the frozen per-dispatch reads. The paper's numbers come from `analysis.json`, which reads the frozen folder alone and reproduces byte for byte.

## Limits

- **Small numbers.** The baseline is eight rows. The forward rows are nineteen or twenty. The area readings are six, over eleven tickets. The cost pairs are three. Each mechanism in Finding 5 rests on five or six cases, and the cases are the ones the blind prompt named.
- **The blind reads are partly circular.** The blind prompt is not in the repository, and not reachable through the API. Its wording is reconstructed from the passes' own method lines, such as batch A's "Judged from the code only", and that is a limit, not a fact. One prompt names the copies, the tidy-after removes them, and the same prompt judges the result. The Periodicals answer this for area direction through `af55cb73`, because they read the same areas without the prompt. They do not answer it for any single change.
- **No like-for-like baseline for areas.** Nothing outside the loop was scored one change per session in the same framing. "Better in both passes" is a statement about the loop's own areas, not a comparison with untouched code.
- **No independent reading after `af55cb73`.** LIN-4292, 4296, 4304, 4306 and 4307 merged after it. Three of the twelve better-in-both readings involve one of them: the pair LIN-4292+4304, and the single readings of LIN-4296 and LIN-4304. Those readings are unchecked by a reader who did not write them.
- **Surveys before 5 November are not in the record.** LIN-4150 holds 50 comments from 5 Nov 02:58 onward, and the 2 to 4 Nov Surveys were pushed out by the cap. For 42 of 59 place tickets the frozen record shows no pre-work Survey. The Surveys for the 4 Nov forward rows are visible only in the notebook's log narration. The proxy cap is a Harbour defect, in the class of LIN-3309 and LIN-4068 as the LIN-4315 ticket thread names them (not frozen here). It is not fixed here, and it is named for John to file.
- **Which way the bias runs.** The circularity favours the tidy-after readings, because the prompt that names the copies is the one that judges their removal. The freeze's gaps (the Surveys before 5 Nov) hide the loop's early evidence, so they cannot make the loop look better than it was, but they do make the record thinner than the loop was. The Periodicals do not share the prompt. They read the same direction and found a worse area, so they are the check on the first bias, and they stop at `af55cb73`. Whether "small, and net better" survives once the circularity is discounted, this paper cannot say: the only non-circular check covers the window to `af55cb73`, and the blind tallies cannot be separated from the prompt that produced them.
- **Cost is live and partial.** Three place rows are partial. A parent's cost excludes its children, so LIN-4255's $56.10 and its pieces' $95.03 are separate figures.
- **Where the notebook and the code disagree, the code wins.** The notebook's 17 / 3 / 1 / 0 is overturned, and this paper replaces it. The ledger's pass-1 figure does not reproduce. The ledger's cost for LIN-4277 is stale. The census is 18 at `1d10f9b3`, not the 22 the ticket's Surveys carried. The research comment listed LIN-4178 and LIN-4181 as partial; the frozen reads show LIN-4173, 4177 and 4178, and 4181 is outside the ledger. The notebook does not record LIN-4307's merge.
- **The Periodicals are instruments, not an audit.** They are written by the same kind of agent that wrote the code. They agree with each other on direction, and they disagree on one area.
- **LIN-4254 is in flight.** The 18 is a count at the pin, not a finish.

## Next

1. **Test John's hypothesis directly.** Larger standalone tidies, chosen from the Periodicals' worst areas (the other-features catch-all, the task-page loader), against the same areas left alone. That is the test that could move the net direction, and this window cannot make it.
2. **Give an area read a baseline.** Put the blind-read prompt into the evidence folder or the repository, and score an untouched area one change per session in the same framing.
3. **Read the merges after `af55cb73` independently** before the essay cites them: LIN-4292 onward, and LIN-4308 onward, with a Periodical run.
4. **Check what the census should guard.** A count of files that still hand-write a fake DOM does not show whether the moved suites share one owner at the pin. Decide whether a guard on the shared thing would replace the count.
5. **Proposal lines for the Library.** Three are added to `proposals.md`: whether a one-owner change that deletes its copies reads better than one that leaves them for a tidy-after (LIN-4253 and LIN-4252 are the first two cases); what a larger standalone tidy does to the worst areas; and whether an area read with a baseline still reads better. Whether a written scope call predicts a finished move is not yet a line. Twenty of 59 place tickets have one, and that is the next thing to count.
6. **Not this paper.** A check over `docs/papers/`, catching up on the unchecked papers, a shared Library freeze helper, and where live experiment records should live. The essay can name them; this paper does not do them.
