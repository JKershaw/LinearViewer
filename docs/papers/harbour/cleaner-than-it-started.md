# Can a task finish with the codebase cleaner than it started?

**Tasks on average: not shown. Areas and finished moves: yes, on few cases.** Over 4 to 6 November the loop ran on 53 place merges across LinearViewer and simple-dispatcher. Its 62 place rows, on 59 tickets, include nine baseline place rows from the ledger's baseline window. On the one like-for-like blind read, the ordinary tickets came out level with the baseline or behind it, and two of them (LIN-4241 and LIN-4212) read worse in at least one pass. Those ordinary tickets did not visibly make the codebase better. Where a move was finished (its planned work merged), or a tidy-after cleaned up after its parent, the area read better in both blind passes: six area readings covering eleven tickets, every pass better and none worse. The Periodicals give a weaker picture. Each reads its own window. Drift & Coherence run 4 reads `2eb73ae0..af55cb73` (29 commits on 5 November) as "better, small"; Seams & Tangles run 8 reads the same 29 commits, gives per-area verdicts and names one loop-touched area that got worse, the other-features catch-all that LIN-4253 grew; Comprehension-Debt run 5 reads from 23 October to `af55cb73` and rates 12 of 21 areas better. The later 4 November rows were read by Seams run 7 and Drift run 3 (`d7ceb373..2eb73ae0`), and Comprehension-Debt run 5 spans all of 4 November; Drift run 4 and Seams run 8 start at `2eb73ae0`. Comprehension-Debt's two worse areas are not caused by a loop change (Finding 3). So, over the windows the Periodicals read, the area readings and the Periodicals point the same way, small and better. That is not a finding about tasks on average. Three limits matter most. The blind area reads ask every change whether it added a second copy and what it deleted, which is the question a tidy-after answers, so they are partly circular. Nothing outside the loop was scored per area the same way, so "better in both passes" is not a comparison with untouched code. And the independent Periodicals read only to `af55cb73`, so the five LinearViewer merges after it have no independent reading. The Limits section lists the rest.

**Terms.** *Harbour* is the product whose codebase this is; LinearViewer is its repository, and simple-dispatcher is the second repository the loop touched. A *ticket* is a Linear issue; a *merge* is a first-parent commit on `main`. The *place* is what a later change has to work within: its owners, copies, lists and guards. A *place row* is a merge that a loop ticket landed as part of its work on the place; the place ledger (`docs/plans/survey/place-ledger.md`) keeps one row per such merge. A *tidy-first* is a small tidy filed and landed ahead of a broad ticket; a *tidy-after* is the follow-on that removes what a parent left behind. A *blind pass* is a reader that scores a commit from its code only, without the ticket, and writes a sign (better, neutral, mixed or worse); its sign is the *Balance* cell of that reader's table. The blind passes are labelled batches A to D (four comments, each with 14 rows) and E to AP (one change or one area each). A *Periodical* is a periodic review report in `docs/reviews/` (Seams & Tangles, Drift & Coherence, Comprehension-Debt), which reads the codebase directly. *Census* is the list of hand-written fake DOMs in the test suite. A *Survey* is the read-only step before a ticket's work, and an *after-answer* is the builder's reply after the work. The *scope call* is the "How to go about it" section of a ticket. The *operator* is the person who ran the Survey loop and kept its notebook, with Claude. The *loop* is the sequence run on each ticket: a read-only Survey before the work, a scope call (written by the person, or by Claude on the person's delegation), the build, and a place reading with the builder's after-answer at merge. An *area* is a parent with its tidy-after or its pieces, read as one unit. A *pass* is one blind reader's scoring of one batch or one change; pass 1 and pass 2 are the two sweeps over batches A to D. The *baseline* is the place rows in the ledger's baseline window (`015aa4c6..5334c81a`): nine rows, four from 3 November and five from 4 November, one of the five being the simple-dispatcher half of LIN-4202. They are compared with the loop's forward rows and are not loop changes.

## Method

The population is every first-parent merge from 4 Nov 00:00 UTC to the two pins: 62 in LinearViewer and 20 in simple-dispatcher, 82 in all. Of those 82, 53 are loop place merges and five are baseline place rows from 4 November (LIN-4178, LIN-4179, LIN-4198 and LIN-4202 in both repositories); the other 24 are nine merges the ledger marks as outside it, 14 Periodical, paper or notebook merges, and one parent issue. The 90 rows add four baseline place merges from 3 November (LIN-4166, LIN-4168, LIN-4173 and LIN-4177) and four UI context rows. The 62 place rows on 59 tickets are therefore the 53 loop merges plus all nine baseline rows. The population is frozen by `freeze.mjs`, which reads LIN-4150 first because the proxy returns at most 50 comments per issue. LIN-4150 returned exactly 50, and the freeze asserts that batches A to D, and all 42 blind passes, are inside them (`lib/providers/linear/index.js@1d10f9b3:1516`). Then it reads each ticket's issue, cost (per dispatch) and dispatch history, and the 73 custom prompts dispatched since 4 Nov, the after-answers among them. Every answer is saved raw with its read time.

The blind reads are the primary instrument. Each pass scores a commit, or a ticket or area, from the code only, in one of three forms: batches of 14 single commits, where batches A and B make pass 1 over 28 commits and C and D make pass 2 over the same 28 in a different order, except that pass 1's batch B row 12 (LIN-4240's LinearViewer commit) was scored together with LIN-4239's simple-dispatcher commit in B3 as one cross-repo unit, as the prompt instructs, so pass 1 has 27 signed units against pass 2's 28 (pass 2 scores them separately, D1 and D10) (`blind-codes.json`); one change per session; and one parent with its tidy-after or pieces per session. The sign is the Balance cell of the pass's own table, copied verbatim, and mapped to +1 for better, 0 for neutral or mixed, and -1 for worse. `code-blind.mjs` does the copying, so no sign was re-judged here. The operator's running tally of 17 better, 3 mixed, 1 neutral and 0 worse, kept in the notebook, was overturned by the blind reads, and the ledger says so (`docs/plans/survey/place-ledger.md@1d10f9b3:127`). The Survey status line in `docs/plans/survey.md@1d10f9b3:5` is replaced here by a pointer.

The Periodicals are the instruments that do not share the blind prompt. Seams & Tangles run 8, Drift & Coherence run 4 and Comprehension-Debt run 5 each ground LinearViewer at `af55cb73`. Drift run 3 grounds simple-dispatcher at `07c88f7` and reads `d8d186c..07c88f7` (`docs/reviews/drift-coherence-review-2026-11-05.md@1d10f9b3:9,17`). That corrects the research comment, which said Comprehension-Debt was the only reader of those commits. The census is read with `git show` at each merge. Costs are the frozen per-dispatch reads. The paper's numbers come from `analysis.json`, which reads the frozen folder alone and reproduces byte for byte. The few counts and ratios that are arithmetic on its keys are shown in the text: 53 is the 62 place rows less the nine baseline rows, and 65% is $7.82 over $11.94.

## Limits

- **Small numbers.** The baseline is eight changes in the blind passes. The forward rows are nineteen or twenty. The area readings are six, over eleven tickets. The cost pairs are three. The Finding 5 pattern rests on eleven cases, six that deleted copies and five that added code beside them, and the cases are the ones the blind prompt's question about copies picks out.
- **The blind reads are partly circular.** The blind prompt is frozen in the evidence folder (`prompts/blind-read-prompt.md`), copied verbatim from the operator's answer on LIN-4315. The circularity remains. One prompt asks every change whether it added a copy and what it deleted; the tidy-after is the change that answers that question, and the same prompt judges its result. The Periodicals answer this for area direction through `af55cb73`, because they read the same areas without the prompt. They do not answer it for any single change.
- **No like-for-like baseline for areas.** Nothing outside the loop was scored one change per session in the same framing. "Better in both passes" is a statement about the loop's own areas, not a comparison with untouched code.
- **"Covered" in the evidence table is loose.** The `periodical_reads` column of `evidence-table.tsv` says "covered" for a LinearViewer row inside at least one Periodical window. For the earlier 4 November rows (before `d7ceb373`) that is Comprehension-Debt run 5 alone, and the frozen column does not say so.
- **No independent reading after `af55cb73`.** LIN-4292, 4296, 4304, 4306 and 4307, five LinearViewer merges, merged after it. (Drift run 3 grounds simple-dispatcher at `07c88f7`.) Three of the twelve better-in-both readings involve one of them: the pair LIN-4292+4304, and the single readings of LIN-4296 and LIN-4304. Those readings are unchecked by a reader who did not write them.
- **Surveys before 5 November are not in the record.** LIN-4150 holds 50 comments from 5 Nov 02:58 onward, and the 2 to 4 Nov Surveys were pushed out by the cap. For 42 of 59 place tickets the frozen record shows no pre-work Survey. The Surveys for the 4 Nov forward rows are visible only in the notebook's log narration. On the LIN-4315 ticket thread's account, the proxy cap is a Harbour defect in the class of LIN-3309 and LIN-4068. It is not fixed here, and it is named for John to file. The research comment's counts, the operator's two dials and three structure cases, and the LIN-3309 / LIN-4068 class all come from the LIN-4315 thread, which is not frozen in the evidence folder.
- **Which way the bias runs.** The circularity favours the tidy-after readings, because the prompt's question about copies is the one a tidy-after answers. The freeze's gaps (the Surveys before 5 Nov) may hide early loop evidence. This paper does not measure which way that cuts; it does make the record thinner than the loop was.
