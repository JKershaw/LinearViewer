### #4 — A named witness discharges only by running it

**Verdict:** Adopt with changes. The rule as written is rejected; its general core (a ticket is not a monitor) should land.

**Strongest objection:** The proposal fixes at discharge time what the template already says must be fixed at authoring time, and it does so by overriding LIN-1579 on n=1. LIN-2515's discharge was legal under *both* LIN-1579 routes: the review named a rollback (`SILENT_START_CAPTURE_MS=0`, the exact env-var-and-safe-value shape `:1070` asks for) and routed to LIN-2595, which `:1069` and `:1156` explicitly list as a valid monitor ("a routed follow-up ticket that owns the watch"). The actual fault is upstream: the paper's own finding is that "one grep for `capture()` across the drivers" would have caught it, so the claim was **testable**, and the misfire guard at `:1069` ("a claim a test COULD have proven is not unprovable-in-principle — it is an untested claim, it stays a gate item") already forbids what the review did. The reviewer misclassified a testable claim as unprovable; no discharge rule at close-out can repair a wrong classification at review, and LIN-898's recorded design principle (prompt-system.md, "the fix is at review-*authoring* time, NOT at discharge time") says so. It also moves the axis LIN-1579 said never to move: "close-out only ever cites a name someone else wrote" (`:1159`); #4 makes close-out re-judge provability. **Evidence thinness:** exactly one miss of seven in one 18-day cohort is this class; the mechanism (ticket-as-monitor) is plausibly a class (close-out follow-ups are 74% never worked, so a routed ticket almost never becomes a watch), but the harm is measured once. "Witness" is not even ledger vocabulary: the templates use it only in the bug template (`:150`) and implementation template (`:953`); the ledger says "real-world check or a manual repro naming its exact distinguishing precondition" (`:1063`, `:1149`).

Second objection, on feasibility: "run it" is frequently impossible before merge. LIN-2515's witness needs "a real logged-out host" on the driver the host actually runs; the close-out session runs in a hosted container. Under #4 that item becomes a hard block, the task cannot set Done, and the only exits are a human naming the precondition or holding the ticket open — the posture `:1176` explicitly rejects ("never as an open task waiting for time to pass") and the one LIN-898 was introduced to relax. `close-out-claims.md`'s LIN-2452 shows what a hard block buys in practice: Done-withheld, then a runner ruling filed the tickets five minutes later and the parent closed anyway.

**Generality:** 2/5 as written, 4/5 reformulated. Teams with riskier changes (data, security) *cannot* run production-only witnesses pre-merge; they use canaries, staged rollouts and alerting — which is the named-monitor lane, provided the monitor fires. "Run it or block" would harm any team shipping behind feature flags or against third-party webhooks: every such change becomes a human-ruling ceremony. What a riskier team *would* want is the weak spot #4 gestures at: `:1069` lets a data-path claim discharge to a monitor "regardless of risk surface", and a ticket is on the list of monitors. Human reviewers routinely accept "we'll watch metric X" and routinely reject "we filed a ticket to watch it".

**What it removes that matters:** The reviewer's authority to declare a claim unprovable and name its monitor (LIN-1579's "who authorizes" invariant). The named-rollback route for runtime-logic items that happen to have a describable witness. More Done-withheld close-outs, which the record shows convert into human rulings rather than into run witnesses. It also needs landing in five places — review `:1067-1070`, close-out `:1155-1159`, meta-prompt `:299` (3a), `lib/completion-signals.js:194`, `lib/prompts/autopilot-kickoff.js:403-408` (the orchestrator restates the lane independently) — and the test at `tests/unit/prompt-templates.test.js:2584` pins that "the LIN-1579 named-monitor/named-rollback lanes remain untouched".

**Reformulation:** Two surgical, general edits at review-authoring time. (1) **A ticket is not a monitor.** Strike "a routed follow-up ticket that owns the watch" from the monitor list at `:1069` and `:1156` (and meta-prompt/completion-signals/autopilot twins). A monitor is a thing that *fires* — log line, metric, loudly-failing path. A ticket may *record* the watch but cannot be the watch; LIN-2595 open and LIN-2611 "never fired" is the proof. (2) **Make the misfire guard a stated step:** before an item takes the unprovable lane, the review writes one line naming why no check short of production could prove it (LIN-2515 could not have written that line honestly — the driver grep existed). Routing stays legal for outside-class items exactly as `:1150` has it.

**Cheapest test:** Read-only proxy sweep, one afternoon: every close-out since LIN-1579 landed (2026-07-26) whose ledger discharged an item by citing a routed follow-up as its monitor; for each, is the follow-up worked, and did anything ever fire? If the majority are Backlog with nothing fired (the 74% figure predicts this), the "ticket is not a monitor" edit is confirmed. Then run the edit for a fortnight and count Done-withheld close-outs and human rulings per close-out against the cohort's 0.3 (review-gated) baseline — if rulings rise, the guard is too strict.

### #6 — A reader for the close-out

**Verdict:** Run as experiment first, targeted, using the reader that already exists. Reject the universal form.

**Strongest objection:** The two false claims were one bug, already fixed in code, and a reader would have cost ~300× the repair. LIN-2773 and LIN-2775 both called `GET /api/proxy/labels?teamId=` (the template at `:1184` names the call without `teamId`), the team-scoped GraphQL filter dropped workspace-level labels, and LIN-2787 (`d0b6403b`, `712565f4`, merged 2026-09-13 as PR #1489) made `labels()` inclusive (`lib/providers/linear/index.js:1695-1710`). The same false claim cannot recur by that path. Repair was $2.84; a reader over the cohort's 192 close-outs at ~$4.6 is ~$880 per 18 days, and `cheap-implementer.md` already shows the subscription "pays twice per ticket" ($8.80 of Opus per cheap-implemented ticket); a reader makes it thrice. The consequence of the false claims was metadata — follow-ups filed without a type label under a step the template marks "best-effort, never blocking" (`:1185`) — not shipped behaviour. **Thinness:** n=2, one root cause, the lowest-severity class in the seven.

Second objection: the reader exists and nobody dispatches it. `retrospective-audit` (`:1213-1263`, LIN-2261) audits "the task's own description or summary/close-out comment… directly against the landed code at HEAD". It was run over 50 tickets on 2026-08-23 for $210.59 ($4.21 each): 8 Request Changes, 0 false dones (`lane-run-review-2026-08-23.md:494-545`). Nothing in `lib/scheduler.js`, autopilot or the meta-prompt routes to it routinely (grep: only `workflow-config.js:38`, `wall-clock-summary.js:47`, `dispatch-factory.js:406`). Its reporting rule says "do not file follow-up tickets" (`:1258`), and the lane-run review's own lesson was that "the retrospective template must own its own filing" because no close-out is coming. So a reader for the close-out has no consumer of its own: a comment on a Done ticket that nobody reads is exactly the narration proposal #7 wants off the ticket. Note the honest counter: that sweep's wave 1 (16 tickets) returned 0 RC and the operator recommended a targeted subset; wave 2 found all 8 in the 34 that targeting would have skipped. That is real evidence against *sampling* — but the population was in-lane-reviewed with no independent review, where the cohort's tickets each carry an Opus verdict.

**Generality:** 2/5 universal, 4/5 targeted. A post-hoc verification that the definition-of-done claims hold is general good practice (release verification, QA sign-off) — always sampled or risk-targeted, never a third full pass on every ticket. No human team re-reads the closing comment; they read the deploy. "Re-run the literal calls a close-out's claims rest on" is fitted to Harbour's proxy-call-shaped close-outs; a team whose close-out is a PR merge and a Slack line has nothing to re-run. Harm: for a team with large, risky changes the reader is a third Opus-priced session per ticket with no authority to change anything.

**What it removes that matters:** Nothing directly, but it spends the one budget the papers say is scarce (gate sessions are 30% of dollars, `review-loops.md`) on the least severe miss class, and it competes with the paper's own finding that the ledger — read 98% — is the one contract that works; a second reader dilutes who owns the close.

**Reformulation:** (1) **Make close-out claims self-evidencing**: any factual claim a close-out makes from a tool call (catalogue contents, snapshot present, CI green) carries the literal request and a response excerpt inline, as the template already demands for CI ("fresh read of the exact commit", `:1126`). Then a later reader — paper, human, or audit — checks for free, and LIN-2773's claim would have shown `?teamId=` in its own evidence. (2) **Target the existing reader**: dispatch `retrospective-audit` only on close-outs that discharged ≥1 inside item by "cited evidence done" — the stratum where a false claim ships a defect (the LIN-2228 "ledger item 7 proven not closed" shape from the sweep), and the stratum `cheap-implementer.md` shows costs most to close ($5+). Give that template the filing authority the lane-run review said it needs.

**Cheapest test:** ~$90, one week: dispatch `retrospective-audit` over 20 cohort close-outs, 10 with non-empty ledgers and 10 explicitly empty. Count false claims per dollar, split by severity (shipped behaviour vs metadata). If the empty-ledger arm finds nothing and the non-empty arm finds LIN-2228-shaped items, the targeted rule is confirmed; if both find nothing, the reader buys nothing and #6 is dead at $90 rather than $880.

### #9 — Review residue does not sink

**Verdict:** Reject as formulated. Adopt the reformulation (state, own severity, aging visibility).

**Strongest objection:** The mechanism cannot work in this codebase, and the lever it pulls has already been pulled and measured. The only automatic pick-up path is the autopilot's "top of the stack" (`autopilot-kickoff.js:440-442, 668`), sorted by `sortIssuesForSwipe` (`lib/render-swipe.js:207-236`): terminal last → boostable Bug → state order → downstream unblocks → critical path → **priority as the final tiebreak**. `isBoostableBug` (`:149-153`) requires `stateType !== 'backlog'` and priority Medium or higher. Residue is filed into Backlog (20 of 23 in `close-out-claims.md`; 770 Backlog/Todo in `tasks-generate-tasks.md`), so an inherited High priority on a Backlog Bug is never boosted and only breaks ties at the very bottom of the sort. Priority presence was already mandated by LIN-2309 (2026-08-25, `6692c3b2`; template `:1183` "derive it from the finding's own risk") and `tasks-generate-tasks.md` measured the result: "only 74 of the 770 carry no priority at all" and review findings still 57% / close-out follow-ups 74% never worked. Changing the value of a field the data says does not discriminate is not a fix. "Picked up by the next lane" is unenforceable from a ticket: a lane is "an ordered list of tickets, handed to you as one continuous mandate" composed by the coordinator (`docs/worker-lane-prompt.md`), and no lane, autopilot or manual text mentions residue (grep: zero hits; `kind:review-residue` appears in no code at all, only in papers). **Thinness:** the direct evidence is 4 of 6 residue tickets open a fortnight on, and one of the four (LIN-2705) is John's recorded acceptance of V1 — a decision, not sinking. The population evidence is real but not residue-specific: human filings sink at 40%, breakdowns 31%, and `root-task-ratio.md` has 786 of 1,340 units never Done.

Does it reverse LIN-2825? Not textually — `:1149` still says inside items discharge by done or drop — but it reverses the *incentive*. Residue is, by `:1182`, outside items or explicitly dropped inside items. Inheriting the parent's priority contradicts the OUTSIDE mark by construction: `:1150` defines outside as "a genuinely separate matter", whose priority is its own. If an item deserves the parent's priority, that is evidence it was inside scope and LIN-2825 says finish it or drop it. And if filing now guarantees pickup at parent priority, "mark outside, file, it will be picked up" becomes a credible way to finish again — the route LIN-2825 closed. The change-log's own recorded counter-argument (`scripts/prompt-template-change-log.md:167-172`) was that close-outs might "reach for an explicit drop more freely than [they] used to file"; #9 makes drop-then-file attractive.

**Generality:** 1/5. Priority inheritance is a known anti-pattern in every tracker — the parent is Done, so its priority is stale, and every child of an urgent parent becomes urgent. GitHub-backed workspaces have no priority field at all (`lib/proxy-instructions.js:595`), so the rule cannot be expressed there. Human teams handle residue with severity triage and aging alerts, not inheritance. Harm: forcing review-found second-order faults (`cheap-implementer.md`: layout, docblock, stale prose) ahead of the operator's ordered lane is precisely the "does a review naming a fault make it more likely to be fixed than a user-found fault" inversion `what-the-reviews-checked.md`'s Next asks about.

**What it removes that matters:** The explicit drop as a real decision (a dropped inside item auto-elevated to parent priority is not dropped). The reviewer's risk-derived priority reasoning at `:1183`, replaced by a worse signal. The operator's lane ordering.

**Reformulation:** (1) **File confirmed-defect residue as Todo, not Backlog**, with its own severity — the stack already boosts non-Backlog Bugs at Medium+ (`render-swipe.js:149-153`), so this is a one-word change to the filing instruction and zero new mechanism. (2) **Put an age on residue, not a priority**: a census of review/close-out-filed Bugs older than N days on the stack digest or `/kpis` — visibility is what human teams use. (3) Keep LIN-2825 intact: residue that matters at parent priority was inside scope.

**Cheapest test:** Zero-cost census now: for every review/close-out-filed Bug in the cohort, cross-tab state at filing (Backlog vs Todo) × priority against picked-up. If Todo predicts pickup and priority does not, the reformulation is confirmed and #9's mechanism is refuted before spending a session. Then a fortnight with the filing instruction changed to Todo for confirmed defects; re-run `tasks-generate-tasks.md`'s never-worked rate on review and close-out filings against 57% / 74%.

### Overall recommendation (A)

None of the three should land as written: #4 and #9 each fix a real fault by reverting a ruling (LIN-1579's lane, LIN-2825's scope rule) on n=1 and n=6, both from close-outs that predate the current template, and #6 prices a universal reader at ~300× the repair it would have prevented. Land the three general forms instead — a ticket is not a monitor (#4), a targeted `retrospective-audit` experiment on non-empty-ledger close-outs (#6), file confirmed-defect residue as Todo with its own severity (#9) — each falsifiable in under two weeks by a read-only proxy sweep before any template changes.

### #1 Review template: cut check narration to conclusions

**Verdict:** adopt with changes. "Move" is right; "PR body" is the wrong slot, and the meta path currently mandates the narration this proposal cuts.

**Strongest objection:** for an LLM reviewer the written table is not a record of the check, it *is* the check. LIN-2274 was institutionalised because "the test exists and asserts something" was being accepted as proof (`prompt-template-defs.js:1035`); the per-mutation record is the forcing function. The paper concedes this ("the work is not wasted — it is what makes the findings trustworthy"). A conclusion-only rule ("17 mutations, 17 red") reopens the door to an unbacked checkbox — `:1044` is already a bare checkbox with no witness demanded. Second objection: "no measured consumer" measured two consumers. Others exist: (a) `retrospective-audit` reads "the PR/commit description, the task's own description or summary/close-out comment" and audits test integrity (`:1247`, `:1251`) — the mutation evidence is its raw material; (b) the papers themselves — `standard.md:40` makes a second paper the designated reader, and three earlier papers consumed mutation narration as evidence (`what-the-reviews-checked.md` on LIN-2515's "19-mutation ledger that caught 18"; `efficiency-levers.md:48` "13 of 14 reverts killed", "27 of 27 green after deleting the code"; `cheap-implementer.md:68`); the papers' instrument is `GET /api/proxy/issues/LIN-n`, which cannot see GitHub; (c) runner summaries quote mutation counts (LIN-2622 `b16bac84`), though #7 wants those gone anyway. Third: the paths already diverge — the handwritten directive attaches "name the specific mutation" to the *failing* case (`:1035`), but meta rule (4) says "record the specific mutation tried and what was observed as a review finding" for every one (`meta-prompt-template.js:298`). #1 must reconcile this, not just trim one path.

Thinness: 10 reviews, one marker, consumed share 43-63% with no length relationship; the "~40%" is the whole narration section (41%), of which ~30% of review words was unread and ~10% was CI re-reading the one-liner replaces.

**Generality:** 4. Any team wants review evidence attached to the change, not the tracker; a human GitHub reviewer already writes it there. Harm shape: (i) teams whose tracker is the audit record (regulated work) lose a single-homed record — the PR body is mutable and its edits are not attributed to the reviewer; (ii) code hosts with no PR object (Gerrit, trunk-based with no PR) — "PR body" is a GitHubism; (iii) here merges are true merge commits (`bf718323 Merge pull request #1500`), so nothing in a PR body ever enters git history; the ticket link is the only durable pointer.

**What it removes that matters:** the on-ticket witness that the mutation check ran (if dropped rather than moved); the papers' ability to re-run `review-consumption.md`'s method through the proxy; the reviewer's evidence stays reachable to `retrospective-audit` only because that template reads the PR too. The PR *body* is the implementer's artefact: a second session overwriting it destroys the implementer's description and un-homes authorship.

**Reformulation:** "Check narration goes on the change, not the ticket. The reviewer posts the mutation table, conformance table and boundary checks as a PR *comment or non-approving review* on the code host (never edit the PR body; never an approving GitHub review — the verdict stays single-homed on the ticket per `:1082`). The ticket comment carries the ledger, the findings, the verdict, one CI line, and one line per check with its count and the URL of the record (`mutation-checked 17/17 red — <link>`). A mutation that survives is a finding and stays on the ticket. Both paths: fix meta (4) to match." Note `:1099` already names only ledger + verdict + CI state as the comment's content, so the change is adding the home and the prohibition, not rewriting the contract.

**Cheapest test:** two arms by ticket parity for a fortnight (odd LIN → new wording; ~6 reviews/day gives ~40 per arm). Measure (a) first-pass approval and close-out cost per the parent paper's method; (b) the drift signal — reviews whose ticket comment claims a mutation count but whose PR carries no record; (c) honesty spot-check — re-run one mutation from five reviews per arm. Any nonzero (b) confirms the forcing-function objection. `scripts/eval-review-closeout.mjs` is the offline pattern if a live arm is unwanted.

### #5 Review class check made terminal

**Verdict:** adopt with changes — reject the wording "list every member with a line citation"; adopt the LIN-1871 form for the review's class check.

**Strongest objection:** the review's class check (`:1023`) is the only class check in the pipeline still in the pre-LIN-1871 shape: it asks for "the class and the unhandled instances" and never for the *bound*. Research (`:281`), plan (`:575`) and plan-review (`:862-864`) all require "how you bounded it and every member the bound found", plus the no-sweep carve-out. The proposal fixes the symptom (a short list) with more list, not with the bound. Test it against its own evidence. LIN-2619: the review grepped `lib/` and `routes/`, cited three readers, and missed `lib/flight-companion-gate.js:593` (`surface = currentSnapshot.attentionCount > 0`) — the members are spread across names (`attention`, `attentionKeys`, `attentionCount`), exactly the "merely awkward to grep" class `:281` says does not qualify as un-sweepable. Three line citations would have looked complete and been wrong; `:283` already names this failure ("a bound that looks authoritative and is quietly incomplete ends the conversation with the wrong answer"). What catches it is a stated query a second reader can contest (`:863`: "one runs it and the answer is on screen"). LIN-2641: the "class" is clauses of one caption string (`lib/effort-readout.js:597`); "every member with a line citation" cites one line twice — the rule does not fit the second of its own two cases. So the evidence is n=1 for the code-member shape, both misses on the same day, both plan-gated, re-find cost $2.88.

Loop risk: plan-review's send-back rule ("a class, never a member", `:862`) has no counterpart in review. "Terminal" as worded, with no send-back rule, lets a reviewer Request Changes per missing member — LIN-1873's cycle transplanted to a gate that today loops rarely (`review-loops.md`: 53 of 247 sent back, 9 twice). Achievability: production-data classes and destinations of new code have no line to cite; `:281`/`:575`/`:864` carve those out, the proposal does not, so a reviewer either manufactures citations or cannot Approve. Length: a 30-row handled-member table is the mutation table's twin — narration nobody reads unless a member is missed — so #5 as worded adds precisely what #1 cuts.

**Generality:** 2 as worded (a line-citation list on a 200-call-site codemod is noise; a human reviewer wants the `rg` pattern and its count). 5 reformulated — "a class you name, you bound, and you show the enumeration the bound produced" is what LIN-1871 already made general for three templates.

**What it removes that matters:** as worded, the "no sweep, here is why" honesty answer at review time, and it invites manufactured completeness against `:283`. Reformulated, nothing.

**Reformulation:** rewrite `:1023` and meta rule (5) to LIN-1871 form: for each class named, state the bound (query + sha, or the reasoning, or "no sweep" naming which shape and what bounded it instead); report every member the bound found against those handled, and the diff; unhandled inside members go to the ledger (already `:1027`, `:1148-1149`); one round, whole class — a review that finds a member names its class and bound and never sends back per member. Define "terminal" as "Approve requires the bound and the diff to be stated for every class named", not "every member cited". Add the checkbox at `:1051` accordingly. Put the handled-member table where #1 puts the mutation table.

**Cheapest test:** retroactive, under a week: pull every review in LIN-2440..2910 whose class section names a class rather than "isolated"; for each, a fresh session states and runs a bound and counts members the review lacked; split by whether the review stated a query. If short sweeps concentrate in reviews with no stated bound, the reformulation is supported; if they occur equally with a bound stated, neither form buys anything. Offline: adapt `scripts/eval-completeness-check.mjs` with LIN-2619 as the gold case (four known readers), arm A current wording, arm B LIN-1871 form, and measure whether the `attentionCount` reader is found. Forward measurement at 2 events per 18 days is not powered inside a month; record a change-log row with direction anyway.

### #8 UI reviews open the page

**Verdict:** adopt with changes — split into two rules; the first already exists and needs enforcement, the second is new and is not about UI.

**Strongest objection:** "open the page" is already on both paths as a guideline: `:1056` "for visual or behavioral changes, verify the result directly where possible", meta rule (2) "take screenshots, run the app, check viewports" (`meta-prompt-template.js:298`). It is not a Review Checklist item (`:1039-1052` has no "verified on the running surface" line) and demands no witness. LIN-2618's reviewers did not follow the existing guideline ("text against text"); a second copy of the same sentence is not a fix. And the second evidence case contradicts the rule: LIN-2621's review *did* open a browser at 390×844 and read computed colours in both themes, and missed `parseDecisionsResult` discarding `count` and `truncated`. So of two misses, "open the page" catches one; the other needs "what does the consumer do with every field the producer returns" — a data-contract check, not a page load. n=1 per rule. Unknown base rate: nobody measured how many of the ~111 reviews on UI tickets already opened the page (`cheap-implementer.md:68` shows Opus reviews measuring "rendered widths" unprompted), so the guideline's absence cannot be blamed. "Minutes" is this repo's price (`npm start`, Playwright installed); for a page behind auth or production data it is a session.

**Generality:** 3 as worded ("the page" is a web-app-ism; "client-side surfaces" is undefined for other stacks, and a checkbox "opened ✓" is false assurance — LIN-2621 ticked it). 5 reformulated: "verify on the running artefact the user meets, cite what you loaded and saw" is universal review practice (pull the branch and run it), and "every producer field accounted for by the consumer" applies to API clients, webhook handlers and CLIs as much as pages — LIN-2621 is a dropped-field bug that happened to be UI. Harm shape as worded: it crowds out the non-UI equivalent (run the CLI, call the endpoint, render the prompt) and adds a per-field table to reviews while #1 cuts tables.

**What it removes that matters:** nothing removed. As worded it narrows Manual Verification to one surface class.

**Reformulation:** (1) Promote Manual Verification to a checklist item with a cited witness: "Verified on the running artefact: <what was loaded, at what URL/command/viewport, what was observed> — or: not run, because <reason>", generalised: page → load it; endpoint → call it; CLI → run it; template → render it. (2) Add a field-complete consumption check as the consumer-side mirror of the ledger's producer item (`:1063` already lists "a producer that must emit an input this change now consumes"): for every consumer the change adds or edits, enumerate the producer's output fields and state what the consumer does with each; a dropped field that carries state the user or caller must know (`truncated`, `count`, an error code) is a finding. Counts and the URL of the record on the ticket; the field table where #1 puts the mutation table. Both paths; pin ordering against `tests/unit/prompt-templates.test.js:1717-1724`, `:4272-4281`.

**Cheapest test:** one week retroactive: for every Done ticket in the cohort whose PR touches `public/` or `lib/components/` (git numstat), mark whether its review cites a load (URL, viewport, screenshot, computed style) and whether it names the producer's fields — the base rate. If page-loading is already above ~80%, rule (1) is redundant and only (2) matters. Then two weeks forward with the checklist item: count witness lines present and dropped-field findings raised; falsified if neither moves.

### Overall recommendation (B)

All three are right about the direction and wrong about the mechanism as worded; each should land as the general rule it is an instance of — evidence on the change not the ticket (#1), the LIN-1871 bound-and-diff form for the review's class check (#5), a cited running-artefact witness plus a field-complete consumption check (#8) — and none as a rule keyed to pages, PR bodies or line citations. Adopt #1 and #8 with the reformulations and a two-arm fortnight; hold #5's wording, adopt its LIN-1871 reformulation, and run the retroactive bound-check first because its forward signal (2 events per 18 days) cannot be read inside a month.

## Report C — proposals 2, 3, 7 (planning and orchestration)

Sources read in full: the brief, the three papers, `review-loops.md`, `prompt-system.md`; templates and readers cited by line below. No files edited, no network calls.

### #2 — Plan as one comment, stop rewriting the description

**Verdict: reject as proposed.** Reformulate (below).

**Strongest objection.** The plan is the one stage artifact that gets *revised in place*, and comments cannot be revised through this proxy. The plan template's revision half says "start from the plan already in the description" and "**Replace, don't append** … via `POST .../description/replace` … The description carries exactly one current plan, never a stack of them" (`lib/prompt-template-defs.js:245-249`). That endpoint exists (`routes/proxy-writes.js:462`); a comment-edit route does not — `updateComment` is in the provider interface (`lib/providers/interface.js:115`, `lib/providers/linear/index.js:1957`) but no proxy route exposes it, and close-out states it outright: "there is no comment-edit endpoint" (`prompt-template-defs.js:1198`). Plan-gated tickets average 1.9 plan-review rounds, so #2 puts two or three full plan comments on every gated ticket and every downstream reader must pick the current one — exactly the stacking failure the Replace rule was written against.

*Editor's note (added after the review):* this sentence is wrong on one fact. `PATCH /api/proxy/issues/:issueId/comments/:commentId` has existed since 2026-08-10 (LIN-1160, PR #1103; `routes/proxy-writes.js:641`). The close-out template's claim at `:1198` is stale and is now its own ticket. The remaining objections below stand.

The proposal also solves no measured harm. The parent paper's headline is that record size predicts nothing; its evidence that reviews use problem/acceptance/anchors is evidence *for keeping the description*, not for moving the plan out of it. Relocation drops zero words (the paper itself: "simplifying it means dropping kinds of comment, not de-duplicating"), and the machine reads comments in full anyway — `formatIssueContext` emits "all comments with full content" with no truncation (`lib/openrouter.js:106-117, 610-625`), so the recommender, brief and recap see the same tokens either way. The stated motivation ("30% still carry an Implementation Plan at HEAD; prune ran on 26%") gets worse, not better: comments are permanent and untouched by the prune, so 100% of tickets would carry the plan at HEAD forever.

**Generality: 2.** Human teams put the spec in the ticket body and the plan in a *linked, versioned* artifact (design doc, RFC, PR description) — neither the description nor a comment. Of the two, a comment is the worse home in any tracker whose comments are append-only from the pipeline's write surface; on Jira (a provider here) it is also the surface nobody reads chronologically. Harm shape: any change that is planned, reviewed and revised more than once.

**What it removes that matters.**
- The single-current-plan invariant (above), and with it a known location for six readers: plan-review "Start by reading the plan in the description" (`:856`), breakdown (`:463`), implementation (`:939`), the meta-prompt routing rule "Read the issue description for an implementation plan" (`lib/prompts/meta-prompt-template.js:191, 197`) and its stated doctrine "COMMENTS: process notes … DESCRIPTION: finalized scope, key findings, implementation plan" (`:320-322`), and the orchestrator's cross-check target "a plan written into the description" (`lib/prompts/autopilot-kickoff.js:481`; `docs/autopilot-operating-manual.md:94`).
- The brief's precedence: "The description is the seed; comments … are amendments. On conflict, the most recent and specific signal wins" (`lib/brief.js:38`). A plan-as-comment is outranked by any later narration comment.
- The two deterministic readers survive only with a stub written *by the plan step*, not just by close-out: `PLAN_MARKER` is description-scoped and its limit 7 says widening to comments is "differential misclassification aligned with the treatment … Do NOT widen the surface back to comments without re-deriving this argument" (`lib/follow-on-ratio.js:76-95, 149`; pinned at `tests/unit/follow-on-ratio.test.js:664, 784-814`); `extractSessionFit(description)` feeds the meta-prompt's routing fact (`lib/recommendation-facts.js:47-52` → `meta-prompt-template.js:94`). The class bounds plan-review re-derives are also told to live "in the issue description … where plan-review will look for them" (`:279`).
- The snapshot archive is *not* lost — it captures comments too, no TTL (`lib/task-snapshot-store.js` header) — but the papers' "peak description" metric would fall by construction, making the change look like a saving that never happened.

**Reformulation.** The general rule is "spec in the ticket body; the plan in exactly one *replaceable* artifact, referenced by a stub (session-fit, gate decision, pointer) in the body." Today the only replaceable artifact is the description, so the fix for the measured fault is to make the prune run (26% → ~100%: it fails on the archive-verify ceremony at `:1191-1193`, not on the edit) — or ship a comment-edit route and pin one plan comment by id. Either keeps every reader above intact.

**Cheapest test.** One week: hand-run ten plan-gated tickets with the plan as a comment plus stub, dispatch plan-review and breakdown on them (~$5/round), and count (a) verifiers that read the wrong or a superseded plan, (b) plan comments stacked after revision, (c) briefs whose `## Current` still carries the plan. Separately, measure the prune's failure mode on the 74% where it did not run (LIN-2913's shape) — that is the cheap fix if it holds.

### #3 — Plan-review gate opt-in by risk surface, not session-fit

**Verdict: run as experiment first — not before 2026-09-26, and only on the (a)-only arm.** Reject the version that drops (a) outright.

**Strongest objection.** This is not a new gate; it is "drop criteria (a) and (b)". The current gate already fires on risk surface — (c) any relaxed validation/contract/guard, (d) credential, merge-rule or dispatch-contract surfaces (`prompt-template-defs.js:306-309`; router twin at `meta-prompt-template.js:199`). What #3 removes is the multi-session criterion (a) and the routed-around-contract-gap criterion (b). And it removes them two weeks after the fix aimed at the very cost it cites: `review-loops.md` found every send-back asked for "a member, not a design"; LIN-1871 (PR #1459, 2026-09-12) changed the send-back rule to class-not-member, with a pre-registered re-read on 2026-09-26 and an expected direction recorded in advance ("plan-review rounds per ticket fall", `scripts/prompt-template-change-log.md:110-140`). The 42-of-46 cohort (31 Aug–18 Sep) is mostly pre-LIN-1871. Changing admission now confounds a measurement the repo's own change-log discipline exists to protect.

The evidence for "no better outcome" is thin and selected. 4 of 46 vs 3 of 75 genuine misses is a one-sided hypergeometric p ≈ 0.25; the parent paper concedes it "cannot separate the gate's effect from its selecting bigger, riskier tickets" (LIN-2641 was a 1,957-line PR; PRs >400 lines are sent back 44% vs 21%). The direct cost is also smaller than framed: plan-review rounds price at a median $4.59 (`what-the-reviews-checked.md`), so ~1.9 rounds is ~$9 of the $49 gap between plan-gated and review-gated tickets; the "13% of words" is 1,759-word verdicts — a verdict-length problem (proposal #1's sibling), not an admission problem. "Cheap when clean" already makes a passing plan one line (`:876`).

Is risk surface judgeable before a plan? The gate decision is already made *after* the plan, from its file list ("With the session-fit answer settled", `:304`), by the planner, self-assessed, with the router taking "due: no" at face value unless the plan visibly contradicts it (`meta-prompt-template.js:199`). Risk surface is equally self-assessed, so #3 neither gains nor loses on that axis.

What happens on the first multi-session ticket with no gate: breakdown carves "one subtask per surface" and copies the arrows (`:463-480`). A class the plan missed becomes a *missing subtask*, invisible to every child's review (each child reviews its own surface) and found only at the parent's class check after all children ship — the LIN-2516 pattern — at the price of a new ticket through the full pipeline versus one $4.59 round. Note the paper's own best shape depends on this: 21 of the 33 lane tickets (zero genuine misses) are breakdown children, and (a) fires for exactly the plans that get broken down. #3 removes the gate from the plans that feed the pipeline's cleanest record.

**Generality: 3.** Risk-surface gating (RFC required for schema/security/API changes) is standard practice and general. But human organisations also design-review large multi-person plans *because* they fan out; dropping (a) harms migrations, multi-repo changes and anything a breakdown distributes, in any team. Dropping (b) means nobody ever checks that a chosen workaround named the contract gap it routes around — plan-review check 2 is the only checker of Strategy Framing.

**What it removes that matters.** The pre-implementation check on plans that become N tickets; the only reader of Strategy Framing's gap-naming; the point where a human weighs in before subtasks are minted (plan-gated tickets drew 1.0 human rulings vs 0.3, and the two longest loops ended by ruling); the checkable record "plan-review due: yes/no, naming which of (a)–(d) fired" (`:311`); and the deterrent effect on plan authoring — the class-bound section is written "where plan-review will look for them" (`:279`), and nothing here measures plan quality when nobody will look. Also: the LIN-1871 re-read becomes uninterpretable.

**Reformulation.** Gate on both fan-out and risk, but make the gate's *depth* proportional: an (a)-only plan gets checks 1 and 4 (class bound, session-fit) under the class-not-member send-back rule; (b)–(d) plans get all seven. Enforce the one-cycle bound mechanically — the router already says a second Request Changes → `blocked` (`meta-prompt-template.js:204-205`) yet 33 of 94 went round two or more times. General form: when a gate's send-backs are member-level, fix the send-back rule before the admission rule.

**Cheapest test.** (i) Free: the pre-registered 2026-09-26 re-read of first-pass approval and rounds per ticket. (ii) One day: classify the 46 plan-gated tickets by which of (a)–(d) fired (recorded in the description per `:311`; pre-prune text is in `/snapshots`) × send-back reason (member / class / design / reconcile-with-landed). If (a)-only plans' send-backs were all member-level, they are LIN-1871's population, not #3's. (iii) Two weeks: LIN-2914-shaped A/B on (a)-only plans, ten gated vs ten ungated, counting missing-subtask discoveries at the parent's review.

### #7 — Narration off the ticket

**Verdict: adopt with changes.** The intent is right; the destination as proposed is not durable, and two of the four comment kinds have named consumers.

**Strongest objection.** "No consumer cites them" is an absence of measurement, not a measured absence. `review-consumption.md` marked what consumes *reviews*; it never sampled autopilot comments as the artifact, and its Limits note the reverse flow exists (LIN-2622's leg summary `b16bac84` cites the review's mutation count). Meanwhile the consumers that do exist are on the record: the verb-override note is designed as "the raw material for closing the 10% gap" (`docs/autopilot-operating-manual.md:154`); the voyage log is "the durable record that survives session death", read by the runner "before assuming today is the passage's first flown cycle" (`docs/passage-runner-prompt.md:71, 165-168`); the run-summary comment is "the experiment's standing evidence requirement" (`autopilot-kickoff.js:217`); and the papers themselves read narration as evidence (`cheap-implementer.md:274` "every event is a comment on LIN-2831"; `never-worked-pile.md:68`; `what-the-reviews-checked.md` Limits, provenance lines). Every LLM read of the ticket also ingests it in full (`openrouter.js:106-117, 610-625`), which is an unmeasured token cost *and* a consumer: the brief is told to "mine comments … for decisions, constraints, and open questions" (`brief.js:39`).

The destination is the real fault. "The dispatch record or oplog": dispatch history expires after 30 days and the queue after 24 hours (`lib/dispatch-store.js:170-171`); there is no `lib/oplog*` module — "oplog" appears only as an example monitor in prompt prose (`prompt-template-defs.js:1069, 1156`) and one code comment (`routes/workspace-api.js:3934`). Ticket comments are permanent, and the snapshot archive stores comments too (`task-snapshot-store.js`), so moving narration off the ticket also removes it from the only durable archive. As written, #7 is "delete narration after 30 days".

Who reads it today: the census does not (observer sweep/pass read dispatch rows, `lib/observer-pass.js`), the Flight Companion posts decisions and does not read narration (`public/flight-companion.js:1021`), and the manual routes the *live* human to the session's one-liners, not the ticket (`autopilot-kickoff.js:606-614`; Live Console at `lib/live-console.js` for 30 days). A human arriving a week later, or after a session died, has only the ticket.

**Generality: 4 for the intent, 3 as proposed.** Bot chatter on tickets is disliked everywhere — GitHub collapses bot comments, Jira teams route bot output to panels; the general practice is one edited status comment or a linked run record. Harm shape: multi-week passages whose next session must re-orient from the record, and post-mortems after retention expires.

**What it removes that matters.** The permanent record of what the orchestrator did and why (the research programme's raw material); the verb-override signal for the heuristic loop; the voyage log's re-orientation role; a human's after-the-fact view once the dispatch record ages out; and the snapshot archive's copy of all of it. The machine loses nothing it reads deterministically.

**Reformulation.** "One status surface per run, not one comment per event; the destination must be at least as durable as the ticket and linked from it." Concretely: keep on the ticket one terminal line per leg (kind, verdict, evidence link); move mid-run cross-checks and beat chatter to the dispatch record *after* its retention is raised or the record is snapshotted; make the verb-override note a structured field on the dispatch item (the override is already recorded there, `routes/proxy-dispatch.js:597-611`); keep voyage logs until passages have a store of their own. Stepped-run beat-completion comments are stage output, not narration (the parent paper says so), and stay out of scope.

**Cheapest test.** (i) One day: for the 184 Done tickets, compute narration's share of the `formatIssueContext` tokens sent per recommend/brief call — the cost nobody measured. (ii) Two days: grep the brief cache (7-day TTL, `lib/brief-cache.js:35`) for Changelog/Open-question lines sourced from run-summary or override comments — the real consumption count. (iii) LIN-2915's fortnight drop, with three counters added: hand-back rulings that cite a run comment, legs that repeat a finished beat (re-orientation loss), and first-pass approval / miss rate.

### Overall recommendation (C)

Reject #2 (it relocates words, breaks the single-current-plan invariant, and answers no measured harm — fix the prune's 26% run rate instead); hold #3 until the pre-registered LIN-1871 re-read on 2026-09-26 and then trial only proportional depth on (a)-only plans, never dropping the multi-session criterion; adopt #7 once the destination is durable and linked, keeping one terminal line per leg on the ticket. None of the three removes a measured harm as written — the general rule across them is: fix the send-back rule and the destination, not the location of the plan.
