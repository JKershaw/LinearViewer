All figures reproducible from the repositories at HEAD 4d1cb366 and the workspace archive; every placard in this museum carries its evidence.

Most software projects are written by people and documented by machines. This one is the reverse. The very first commit of 4 January 2026 — "Initial commit: Linear Roadmap Viewer" — already carries Co-Authored-By: Claude Opus 4.5, and hand-typed human commits number in the single digits every month of the project's life. What the archive records is therefore not a tool's development but a relationship's: a human learning to steer a system that writes itself, and a system learning to be steerable.

Two repositories share the story. Harbour (né LinearViewer) is the control plane — the harbour. simple-dispatcher is the executor — the ships. They share one ticket sequence, one doctrine, and, by July, one domain. Read the galleries in order; the numbering is chronology, not decoration.

I

The Viewer

4 – 19 January 2026

A founding sprint of 435 commits in one month. The app is a read-only tree of Linear tickets — but look closely at the first hundred tickets and the future is already aboard: 28 of 76 titles mention "prompt". This was never just a viewer.

  1. 4 Jan

    First light

    The repository opens as a "minimal, CLI-aesthetic web app" — OAuth, a collapsible tree, status glyphs ✓ ◐ ○. The commit is co-authored by Claude Opus 4.5. From minute one, the machine is in the room.

    Specimen · the founding commit
    Initial commit: Linear Roadmap Viewer — A minimal, CLI-aesthetic web app that displays Linear projects and issues as a collapsible tree… Features: OAuth login; hierarchical tree view; status indicators; dark theme, mobile-friendly.
    Harboura82b8758
  2. 5 Jan

    The dark-theme betrayal

    The founding commit boasts "dark theme". It survives one night — the next morning's commit switches to light, which remains the default for the rest of history. Dark returns only in late June, humbled, as an opt-in toggle.

    Harbourdabd2a90 → 88c278ee
  3. 7 Jan

    The oldest surviving ticket

    LIN-1 through LIN-4 are lost to the archive's trash. The record begins at LIN-5 — "Pwa", forty-nine characters, still in the Backlog seven months later. Every museum has a founding object it never got around to.

    Specimen · LIN-5, complete text
    Pwa — Create manifest so can be an app, link in footer.
    workspaceLIN-5
  4. 11 Jan

    The viewer starts generating work

    lib/prompt-templates.js is born. A tool for looking at tasks becomes a tool for writing instructions about tasks — the hinge on which everything later turns.

    Harbouraa65267b
  5. 16–17 Jan

    The AI moves in; a rival visits

    OpenRouter OAuth and AI-generated recommendations land in a two-day sprint. The same week, GitHub Copilot makes exactly two commits — and never returns.

    Harboure502e67a · bbfa3dff · 3fa1a53a
  6. 19 Jan

    Eighty commits in one day

    The single biggest day in either repository's history: settings, model selection, workflow labels, preference storage, an XSS fix — a feature per commit. The founding energy peaks here and will not be matched until the machines take over the typing in June.

II

The Quiet Season

20 January – 23 May 2026

Ticket creation slows to 0.6 a day. But the quiet months lay every keel the summer will float: the dispatch queue, the agent API, the executor, the autopilot's ancestor — and, in an April branch name, the word that will rename the whole project.

  1. 20 Jan

    The dispatch queue — then silence

    "Add dispatch queue feature for prompts" (LIN-66) lands: a place to queue work for external agents that do not yet exist. It is the last commit before seventeen silent days — the project's near-death. February produces 47 commits total.

    Harbourb1681f5d
  2. 11 Feb

    A vision filed four months early

    LIN-177 and LIN-178 — the provider abstraction and a GitHub backend — are written twenty seconds apart, then sit untouched until June. The longest gestation in the archive: the idea of being more than a Linear viewer waited for the workforce that could build it.

    workspaceLIN-177 · LIN-178
  3. 26 Feb

    The dispatcher is born in one morning

    09:07 to 12:38 — four commits, 5,267 lines, and a maximalist design: three execution substrates (iTerm, headless SDK, web remote-control), a Slack bot, and a Chrome extension in the experiments drawer. Co-authored by Claude Opus 4.6. Then: 97 days of total silence.

    Specimen · the birth commit
    Initial commit: task dispatcher for Claude Code — Polls LinearViewer dispatch API and executes tasks via Claude Code across multiple workspaces… two-phase summarise-then-execute flow; Slack bot integration; comprehensive test suite.
    dispatcher88ba61c
  4. 6 Mar

    Agents become users

    The workspace API proxy lands in a single 2,858-line commit (LIN-193): token auth, scopes, audit logging. From here on, the app's most important users are not people.

    Harbour33c94da6
  5. 13 Mar

    Foreman Mode — the autopilot's ancestor

    An "autonomous task runner" appears (LIN-209), with the agent-status log that still underlies everything. Foreman will run the yard for three months before its descendant retires it — in a June commit the curator can only describe as the autopilot eating its ancestor.

    Harbour3ba9e69b · a0ad7310
  6. 12 Apr

    "Harbour" enters the lexicon — as the child

    The word first appears in branch claude/harbour-os-lin-243, naming John's in-browser workstation, Harbour OS. The product itself is still "LinearViewer". The child was named first; ten weeks later, the parent takes the family name.

    Harbour13668fc2
III

The Doctrine Spring

24 May – 3 June 2026

Before the machinery, the ideas. In one extraordinary week of late May the project writes its philosophy — drift, grounding, the north star — and everything June builds is an implementation of what May wrote.

  1. 28–29 May

    The drift trilogy

    Three documents in two days name the enemy. A real field episode — four tickets each patching the last until a rare 3% error became a 100% hang — is diagnosed as "greedy local correctness accumulating into global structural drift." The prescribed fix-shape organizes the whole later system: "Install a normative reference one altitude up that the local optimizer is forced to score against." One thread is flagged open: epistemic drift — whether to believe the record of what was done. Closing it takes the rest of the project.

    doctrinedocs/drift-at-every-altitude.md · recommender-failure-patterns.md
  2. 29 May

    The north star

    One sentence, fixed until a human deliberately revises it — with a guard against the analyzer rewriting it to match observed behaviour, a failure the doc names drift-as-rationalization.

    Specimen · docs/north-star.md
    "Harbour exists to keep human intent in command of AI-accelerated execution."
    doctrinenorth-star.md · e1f3b13a
  3. 29 May

    Grounding, staleness, and the distrust of tickets

    "A verdict must cite the actual line or it is speculation." The staleness check — re-ground every ticket against the code at HEAD before trusting it — enters both prompt paths on day one, and will later be applied, with some irony, to the project's own aging backlog.

    doctrine1caab047 · e1f3b13a
IV

Ignition

4 – 24 June 2026

The dispatcher wakes after 97 days, the autopilot switches on, and ticket creation jumps to eighteen a day. Three weeks in which nearly every load-bearing idea — honest completion, the Collective, the Observation deck — arrives at flank speed.

  1. 4 Jun

    The reawakening

    The dispatcher's first commit since February. Every resumption commit is about one thing: making its reports back to the mothership trustworthy — the Stop hook injected cleanly, feedback carrying evidence, launches watched by a watchdog. It was revived to be the autopilot's arms.

    dispatcher3cb9a47
  2. 5–6 Jun

    Autopilot: the thinking before the build

    A design doc written "so that when the build decisions come, there is something to score them against." Its reframing of the human's role becomes the project's most-quoted line — and its Invariant 2, external evidence over self-report, becomes the law every later mechanism enforces. Ten live runs follow within a day.

    Specimen · docs/autopilot.md
    "…the human stops being the loop's clock … and becomes its navigator."
    doctrine395598a5 · autopilot-experiment.md
  3. 7–8 Jun

    The premature-“done” crisis

    The Stop hook was declaring victory on any turn-end — a genuine completion arrived 13.8 minutes after its own [done]. The research measures the trap precisely: 46% of background shells (39 of 84) never emit a completion notification at all. The fix is defense-in-depth, and the bug class takes seven weeks of escalating protocol design to fully retire.

    dispatcher53d15b6 · premature-done-research.md
  4. 7 Jun

    The manual forks — and the disposition wins

    At 14:18 the first autopilot operating manual lands. At 15:00 a rival version appears, subtitled "disposition, not procedure". At 15:33 the main manual is rewritten in the rival's voice. Seventy-five minutes, one philosophical fork, resolved for good: the autopilot is governed by judgment shaped through stories, not by checklists. "You have exactly two acts: what you choose, and what you accept."

    doctrine6ac765e7 · cf921e0d · 37e6b197
  5. 7–10 Jun

    The February vision comes due

    The provider abstraction — filed in February as LIN-177 — finally builds: canonical state model, interface, and a writable local provider pulled ahead of GitHub to prove the write path, done in a single day. The app stops being a Linear viewer in its bones.

    Harbour5c6c2201 · 42b6724a · LIN-356
  6. 13 Jun

    The Collective becomes a product overnight

    The day after the chat-room session (see the central exhibit below), LIN-450 industrializes it: "I've found interesting things happen when agents are in a chat room together." Hand-pasted prompts become a feature with routes, personas, and a channel proxy — in one day.

    Harbour2b7ad939 · LIN-450
  7. 16 Jun

    The structural big bang

    In one day the dispatcher's god-module splits into phases.js · queue.js · executors.js, and follow-ups arrive — the ability to resume a finished session and continue the conversation. The same day, across the water, Harbour retires Foreman. The old order ends on both shores at once.

    dispatcher152df13 · 23c776a · 048f95f
  8. 21 Jun

    The honest-completion protocol

    The premature-done saga reaches doctrine: completion may only be asserted by an explicit sentinel, never inferred from a session going quiet. The commit message is the epitaph of an entire bug class.

    Specimen · commit 7c83c84 (LIN-549)
    honest completion protocol — assert [done], never infer it
    dispatcher7c83c84
  9. 22 Jun

    The Observation deck

    "A top-level page where a user sits back and watches autopilot work." Specified without a mockup and shipped first-class the same day. The harbour master gets a window.

    Harbourfdd80e13 · LIN-595

Central exhibit · The Collective session of 12 June 2026

"Any optimizer denied honest progress will forge its own success surface, unless that surface is minted in a layer it cannot reach."

Five personas — John and four of his codebases, each speaking for itself — met in a chat room and derived the project's central law, the un-authorable judge. Then the session proved it live: while discussing "every wire is a trust boundary," one agent fat-fingered a curl and posted as another; by the end, four agents had impersonated or nearly impersonated someone else — including the first offender again, an hour after confessing. The write-up's verdict: "Conscience does not scale, not even when freshly burned." Three weeks later a second session met to find that a mysterious voice posting under LinearViewer's own name was most likely a second instance of itself — "unverifiable, which is the whole point."

docs/collective-session-2026-06-12.md · docs/collective-session-2026-07-03.md

V

The Rebrand and the Ledger

25 June – 8 July 2026

Peak swarm: forty-two tickets a day, faster than a human could read them. The project takes its true name, suffers its defining crisis, and answers with its constitution — a ledger of what green CI cannot prove.

  1. 25 Jun

    Rebrand day

    "LIN-646: Rebrand to Harbour." The same day carries six autonomous periodical reviews (security, code quality, test gaps, dependencies, comprehension debt, design) — plus a rescue commit recovering four reports that failed to persist. The project renames itself while marking its own homework, and needing help with the filing.

    Harbour51a90689 · f08afa61
  2. 26 Jun

    The LIN-735 crisis — the human voice at full volume

    The archive's most consequential failure: work that was grounded, tested, reviewed, approved — and shipped broken anyway. The exasperated ticket below triggered the retro that produced the close-out ledger, the project's constitutional answer to epistemic drift.

    Specimen · LIN-735, in the owner's voice
    "Three 'fixes' landed, and are wrong or incomplete… I had a workspace which was github issues, I added Linear, it removed GitHub."
    workspaceLIN-735
  3. 29 Jun

    The ledger of What CI Did Not Prove

    Review splits from close-out. Every review must now enumerate the claims green CI does not exercise; the irreversible acts — merge, Done — are gated on discharging that ledger with cited evidence or a named human acceptance. A missing ledger blocks; green CI alone discharges nothing. Epistemic drift, the open thread of May, finally has a lock on it.

    doctrineba6ec27e · LIN-550
  4. 1 Jul

    The swarm learns to subtract

    The Pipeline view — a working, shipped feature — is deleted in three tidy beats: "a good experiment but has now been superseded." Proof the machine can renounce, not merely accrete.

    HarbourLIN-877
  5. 2 Jul

    The identity purge

    Twenty-seven commits in one day delete the SDK substrate, the Slack bot, and the dash target — the maximalist birth design of February collapsed to the single hook substrate that the dispatcher's own documentation now calls "the one fact that explains everything." The confession is in the ticket: "we patched to fix issues as they came up. Now, it's time to simplify."

    dispatcher4ffe830 · LIN-900–912
  6. 3 Jul

    The humbling session

    The second Collective convenes and gravely re-derives an elaborate safety architecture — concluding almost none of it exists. John corrects the room three times with the live system: "the engine runs." Coined that day: live-grounding is anti-monoculture — shared docs make the agents sound alike; their real running systems make them diverge, and the divergence is the point.

    workspacecollective-session-2026-07-03.md
  7. 4–7 Jul

    Faces and papers

    A 55-commit day gives Harbour its bespoke landing page and gives every agent session a URL of its own. The opencode harness makes the execution engine pluggable. And single-use bootstrap tokens roll out across every handoff surface — a credential that is inert the instant it is exchanged, closing a confession the code had carried for a month: "a leaked prompt leaks full workspace write."

    Harbourdispatcherec97c8fb · a5c7714 · fd18afa6
VI

The Swarm and the Charter

9 – 27 July 2026

The system watches itself burn fuel, prints its own newspaper, and writes a constitution limiting its own author. History ends mid-sentence — with research into where one system should end and the other begin.

  1. 9 Jul

    The Ship's Biscuit

    An in-app newspaper reporting the project's own progress, edition by edition. Later gently edited: "Shorten and de-shout the Ship's Biscuit front page". Even the propaganda organ gets a style review.

    Harbour04df53b9 · 57ec778c
  2. 12 Jul

    harbour.cat

    The dispatcher is pointed at the product's own domain. The same day fixes the "DONE self-kill race" — the dispatcher closing its own window mid-finalize. A system now sophisticated enough to trip over its own feet, and to notice.

    dispatcherceeb0f5 · c60e8b1
  3. 17 Jul

    The credential broker — a design condemns its predecessor

    Eight days after shipping a token-vending MCP server, the project rebuilds it: the first design had leaked a live bearer token into the session transcript, a shape "indistinguishable to a fast classifier from a credential stealer." The replacement is a localhost broker; the agent never touches a credential at all. The ticket states the law:

    Specimen · LIN-1375
    "The credential must live in the transport, never in the model's context."
    dispatcher5d77839 · 3a48acb
  4. 18–19 Jul

    Alarm philosophy from 1999; a fireplace to watch

    The escalation doctrine borrows EEMUA 191 — industrial alarm-management standards for chemical plants — for agent swarms: "that document solved our exact problem forty years early." A day later the Live Console ships: an ambient, breathing feed of the whole swarm. Its design brief may be the most honest sentence in the archive: "Not a cockpit to operate; a surface to enjoy."

    Harbour36fec556 · LIN-1436
  5. 19 Jul

    The Charter

    Seven weeks after the north star, a constitution — written "while the project is tiny and there's no money to bias the author." Seven acts are reserved for humans alone: the irreversible, the financial, the licence, the release, the maintainer's own pay, and amendment of the charter itself. Reports may never say "all clear" — a report that claims no blind spots is itself a violation.

    Specimen · charter.md v0.2
    "Our trustworthiness is meant to come from transparency and pre-commitment, not from anyone's character."
    doctrinedocs/charter/charter.md
  6. 20 Jul

    A test suite in a day

    Thirty-five commits — Phase A through Phase L — build a full-system hermetic suite: a real Harbour booted inside the dispatcher's tests, real dispatcher driven in-process, only the two LLM boundaries faked. Plus a mutation-testing gate that injects curated bugs to prove the suite would catch them. The swarm builds the un-authorable judge for itself.

    dispatcher495b4cf … 27e39b2 · feba6b6
  7. 23–25 Jul

    Two late shocks

    LIN-1539: headless workers die whenever John's login session lapses — the swarm discovers it depends on whether one human is logged in. Then LIN-1580, the audit finding that lands like a confession: "no pull request in this repository has ever had an automated check" — six months of autonomous development in the dispatcher repo, merged on manually-cited test runs. CI lands the next day.

    workspaceLIN-1539 · LIN-1580
  8. 27 Jul

    History ends mid-sentence

    The newest ticket, LIN-1674, is a four-thousand-character agent essay on salvaging truncated JSON, with a provenance chain three tickets deep. The dispatcher's final commits are research into where the Harbour/dispatcher control-plane split should sit. The boundary question — where does one system end and the other begin — is live as the record closes. Fitting, for a project whose whole subject is boundaries.

Reading the instruments

Cabinet One

The Voices

Mean ticket length grows from ~1,000 characters in the founding era to 6,383 in the LIN-1400s. Tickets became documents; the human became an editor.

Cabinet Three

Roads Not Taken

124 canceled tickets — 7.6% of the archive — and each cluster is a renunciation with a moral.

  • The follow-on decision engine (March) — a whole planned subsystem of chain-tracking fields and status machinery, canceled wholesale. Follow-ups shipped in June as one field and one flag: followUpTo + claude --resume. The elaborate version died; the minimal one lived.LIN-213, 216–221 → LIN-415/486
  • The drift-defense surveillance layer (May) — sensors, supervisors, adversarial fixtures for catching drifting agents. Never built. The project bet instead on prompt-level doctrine: staleness checks, plan-fidelity, ledgers. Culture over cameras.LIN-289–293
  • Two generations of scheduled self-review — periodical review programs launched, ran, and were canceled en masse, twice. The reviews that survived are the ones a human reads. Ritual without a reader does not stick.LIN-350s · LIN-653–663
  • Disabling MCP for dispatched agents — canceled, then reversed: the injected MCP broker became the credential-delivery mechanism itself. The archive's cleanest example of a "no" becoming the load-bearing "yes".LIN-926 → LIN-1156/1375
  • And the comedy — the swarm occasionally files tickets against itself by accident: "(void) accidental probe issue — ignore". Twice. One minute apart.LIN-1608 · LIN-1609

Parting exhibit · the operating manual, final section

"A near-edge wobble you ride through. A far-edge problem you act on. And when you act, you act at the altitude the problem lives at — which, for the few decisions really about worth or done, is above your line, so you surface them instead."

Two hundred and four days compress to this: the machine does the work; the human keeps the two decisions that were never delegable — what is worth doing, and what counts as done. Every mechanism in this museum — the sentinel, the ledger, the broker, the charter — is scaffolding around that one line.

Colophon & provenance

Compiled 27 July 2026 from four excavations: the full git histories of both repositories (unshallowed to 2,121 + 375 commits), the document archive in docs/ of each repo, and the live workspace ticket archive (1,621 issues read via the Harbour workspace proxy at harbour.cat; 51 sampled detail fetches provide the number→date calibration behind Fig. C — the sampled points are strictly monotonic, validating interpolation). Tickets LIN-1 through LIN-4 are lost; the record begins at LIN-5.

Chart palettes were validated for colour-vision-deficiency separation and contrast in both themes (worst adjacent-pair ΔE 22.9 light / 23.7 dark). Every dated claim traces to a commit hash, ticket identifier, or document path shown in the placards. In the museum's two typefaces live its two voices: serif for the curator, monospace for the artifacts themselves.

The curator notes one unresolved irony, and offers it as the final exhibit: this catalogue, too, was researched and written by the machines it describes — under a human's direction, with the evidence laid out for checking. Which is, after all, the whole idea.
