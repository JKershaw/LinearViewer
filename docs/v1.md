# Harbour v1: one stranger, one task, one proven merge

*(the scope of the first version other people can use: drafted with agents from John's words, accepted only by the human, versioned, provenance recorded. The north star is the rule; this page is the scope. When they disagree, the north star wins and this page is revised.)*

## Why now

In John's words, 19 September 2026: *"what I'm currently doing is building my ideal product, whereas now I want to make it so that other people can start using it; that's where Harbour really gets interesting."* And: *"this feels like the first time I can really see a clear idea of what this finished version 1 effectively is."*

The papers of the same day say where to meet those people. Newcomers arrive at the ceiling the tools already offer, which has been "hand over one task" for a year; the gate they name is the cost of checking the work; adoption spreads by seeing a colleague's run; and provenance is the one thing shown to make checking cheaper. v1 is built on those four findings and on nothing that contradicts them.

## The bar

`docs/north-star.md`: *"Login → connected → first evidence-verified merge, without talking to the operator, is that step's bar."*

v1 is done when five people outside the operator's circle have reached that bar on their own backlogs, unaided.

## Who, and the job

A developer with a backlog in GitHub, Jira or Linear who wants one task done and proven. They may be hands-on today, saving the prompts that work, or they may arrive expecting to hand a task over. v1 offers the handover first and the prompt underneath it, so both find their step on the first screen.

## The golden path

This is the whole of v1. Everything else on this page serves it or is out.

1. **Log in.** In John's words: *"they arrive on harbour.cat, they log in with their email address."*
2. **Connect.** *"They connect their GitHub repos and their Jira for work."* A tracker and a repo.
3. **See the work.** *"They immediately see a lovely interface that clearly shows their work … or be shown the top of the stack for a task."* The top of the stack is on the first screen; the views are there to explore, not to get through.
4. **Press Go on one task.** *"There's just a great big button that basically says Go."* Go runs one task, bounded, and hands back. It is not the standing loop. The steps of the run show as buttons, in John's words: *"a series of buttons showing the expected steps; run each one and see the results, confirm them, or let them automatically confirm."* Pressing each step is handing over one task and watching. Letting them confirm is ratifying a run. The person chooses, and Harbour records which they chose.
5. **Watch.** *"Just sees a lovely interface of the work in progress."*
6. **Get the PR with its ledger.** *"Essentially just gets a PR at the end along with the ledger, all wonderfully displayed, and can easily understand the task, how it was run."* The display exists to make checking cheap, not to make the run look good: what CI proved, what it did not, what the review read, each row one click from its evidence.
7. **Click merge.** The click is theirs and it is the only path to main.
8. **Share the link.** *"They can share these links like a CI/CD pipeline on the web."* One run, one URL, readable by whoever holds it.

Under the button at step 4: **copy the prompt.** The grounded next prompt for the task, free and unlimited, for the person who wants to do it by hand. This is the rung-3 entry the chain under LIN-614 serves; v1 keeps it and puts it second.

## The measure

- Time from login to first Go.
- Share of people who reach the merge click, and where the rest stop.
- Which mode each person used: copied the prompt, pressed each step, or let the steps confirm. This is the entry-rung measurement the papers could not find anywhere else, taken on Harbour's own users.
- False-escalation rate on the run page: every time it asks the person something, was the answer "why was I asked this?"
- Cost per verified task on the v1 lane, in the money actually spent.

## In and out

**In:** the eight steps; the prompt under the button; one runner that serves people who are not the operator; a ring-fenced free tier; the hardening gate before anyone outside the operator's circle presses Go.

**Out, as later steps rather than never:** organisations and colleagues beyond the share link (*"you add organisations, colleagues can share and watch the same thing"* is the step after this one); machine and model configuration (*"later they can specify things about the machines, the models"*); passages and the standing loop for users, which stay the operator's; periodicals for users; Harbour OS.

## Hypotheses, stated as hypotheses

- **Execution.** Runs execute in the cloud on a runner Harbour operates, with cheap models through OpenRouter on the work-shaped legs and the frontier only where judgement is bought. John: *"even if right now you just plug in OpenRouter and everything runs through the cloud, that's still a really good product."*
- **Pricing.** The free tier is unlimited copyable prompts plus a small, published number of cloud runs. The paid tier is more runs on a small monthly subscription, or the person's own key. John: *"potentially the cloud is the $5 subscription and you get copyable prompts, or something like that; we need to figure out the detail."* The number is decided after the first stranger merges, not before.
- **Entry.** Most arrivals press Go rather than copy. Unmeasured by anyone; the measure above is how v1 finds out.

## What exists and what is missing

As of `8a7f9c6e` on `main`. Each row names where the gap already has a ticket.

| Step | Exists today | Gap for v1 |
|---|---|---|
| Log in with an email address | Linear OAuth, GitHub App installation ("Continue with GitHub"), Jira, personal access token (`docs/architecture/auth.md`) | Email login does not exist. Decide whether "Continue with GitHub" is the v1 door or an email door is built. |
| Connect a tracker and a repo | Providers for Linear, GitHub, GitHub Projects, Jira and local; the GitHub App's repo picker | The connect flow as one uninterrupted path (LIN-614). |
| See the work, top of stack first | The stack (`lib/task-stack.js`) and the views | The first screen: top task and the button, views behind it (LIN-2928's once-over, re-aimed at this screen). |
| Press Go on one task | A scoped run exists: `POST /api/proxy/autopilot/kickoff` with an issue and a task budget, proven on three tickets on 19 September | Steps as buttons with confirm-each or confirm-all; the budget enforced and shown (LIN-2934); a stalled worker reported as stalled (LIN-2932); a failed press that says why (LIN-2933). |
| A runner for people who are not the operator | Every run today executes on the operator's machine; the dispatcher already routes opencode through OpenRouter | The critical path. A Linux host running the dispatcher (LIN-1781, LIN-1785) and machines as account objects (LIN-2883). |
| Watch the run | The Observation view and session pages, built for the operator | The first-user shape of one run: the steps, their state, the evidence as it lands. |
| The PR with its ledger | The review ledger and the close-out that consumes it | The display, one click from each row's evidence. |
| Click merge | Close-out owns the merge today | For a user the click is theirs; close-out stops at the PR. |
| Share the link | Nothing | A run URL readable by its holder. |
| Free tier | Daily prompt limit per workspace (`lib/free-tier-store.js`) | Extend from prompts to runs; publish the number as a ring-fenced line item. |
| Paid tier | Nothing | Billing, after the free tier has produced a merge for a stranger. |
| Hardening gate | The security review periodical; the north star's rule that secure defaults assume hostile input | Per-user token scoping, repo content treated as untrusted, the merge click as the only write to main, reviewed before the door opens. |
| The measure | LIN-1644, time-to-trust instrumentation | Build the funnel and the mode record before the first stranger, or the first five teach nothing. |

## Order

Risk first, polish last, because the polish is the least uncertain thing on the list.

1. The runner for strangers.
2. Go as a bounded one-task run behind the button, with the three reporting fixes.
3. The run page, the PR with its ledger, the merge click, the share link.
4. The funnel and the mode record.
5. The free tier for runs, then billing.
6. The hardening gate.
7. Five people, friends before strangers, watched while they try it. Then the paper on what they did.

Organisations, colleagues and configuration come after the fifth merge, which is the first step's bar met.
