How one task moves

Each box in the third row is a fresh agent session that reads the ticket, does one stage and leaves. Harbour chooses the next stage each time. The numbered steps are the ones Ruling 3 of the plan asks you about.

Numbers are the rows of Ruling 3: 1 tidy-after, 2 a plan and one plan review, 3 further plan reviews, 4 the mutation check, 5 close-out filing follow-ups, 6 the research session, 7 the two Surveys, 8 the blind read, 9 the cut rule and the seal on the ask. Row 10, my own supervising session, sits outside this picture: it starts runs and relays by hand.

What holds what

Harbour keeps three kinds of thing: the job, the place, and the people and keys. The job is held as data. The place is held as text: a comment at the top of each file, a source map in the docs, and the area notes. Your idea of holding the place as data, on a par with tasks and code, would change the middle column.

What this chart cannot tell you

  • Whether a part's purpose is still the right one. Each file says what it is for, and the chart quotes it. The chart cannot say whether that is still what you want. The one-line summary of each area is mine.
  • What is used. Nothing in the repository records which features people use.
  • The exact share of an area on the route. Those splits are by filename and could be off by thousands of lines.
  • Every dependency. Import counts are static imports between source files. Browser scripts are loaded by script tags, so they show no imports at all.
  • The tests. Line counts leave them out. Unit-test lines appear in each area's detail.
