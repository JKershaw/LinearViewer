# Review: from "Like a skilled developer" to round 1, and what comes next

8 November 2026, after round 1 (LIN-4344). I read the whole thread in date order: the papers from 3 October, the survey plan, the log, the round notes, the ledger, the area notes, the Stability Review and the north star. The reading notes are in `review-notes.md`, beside this file.

## The goal

- **The question:** can a task finish with the codebase cleaner than it started? Harbour should carry more of the loop, so the hand carries less.
- **Your tipping point (4 Nov):** when a task, on average, leaves the codebase even a little better, we're in a strong place. Bigger improvements not tied to a ticket can come from the Periodicals.
- **Your two dials (5 Nov):**
  - "make no new mess", always on;
  - "how much to tidy", the person's budget for each task.
- **Convergence:** Harbour should converge work, even slowly, and resist spiralling unless it's tidying up. Harbour's goal is simple code.
- **The bigger aim:** Harbour's value is making agents accountable, not capable (1 Nov): keep the ask intact, judge independently, land with proof. And your trig point: bring codebases toward settled, "including our own".

## The story, in five phases

1. **The diagnosis, 3 Oct to 2 Nov.** Four papers found one problem from four sides:
   - the stages see the cause and write it down instead of fixing it;
   - prescriptive loops spiral;
   - the ask is lost at the first hand-off;
   - nobody's job is the place.

   What had worked was a session doing the resident's work (October's coherence loop and Theseus rounds). It stopped when your attention moved elsewhere.
2. **The Survey, 2–3 Nov.** A read-only forecast of "why is this difficult?", written before any work, named where live changes would bind and what a plain build would leave behind. The open question beat the structured menu. Asked afterwards what the ticket should have been, builders named one owner or one seam 14 times out of 14.
3. **Acting on it, 3–4 Nov.** Rounds 2 and 3 wrote each forecast's preparation onto the ticket, and builders' friction became small tickets that fixed it. Then the first whole-codebase measure found the catch: locally right, globally tangled. Each consolidation made a new hub with no home, and new copies.
4. **The correction, 4–6 Nov.** My own tally said the loop made tasks leave the codebase better. Two blind scorers, reading only code, disagreed: ordinary tickets read no better than before the loop. What did read better was finishing a move:
   - a tidy done first that deletes all its copies;
   - a small tidy-after of the edges a blind read names. Five of five areas turned better this way.

   Three independent Periodicals agreed: "better, small". The paper's answer: "Tasks on average: not shown. Areas and finished moves: yes." The essay's fourth version showed what a plain brief and the right model do.
5. **Round 1, 7–8 Nov.** Six jobs the hand used to do moved into Harbour:
   - the sealed ask;
   - cuts named at scoping, plus the size record;
   - leftovers and area notes;
   - model facts from the OpenRouter catalogue;
   - a rate limit per credential;
   - Periodicals that mint nothing.

   Five of six areas ended better. The spiral (six tickets to about seventeen) and the unusable size setting both came from this session. The Stability Review said the project isn't settling.

**How we've worked:**
- Surveys before work.
- The size of the move set by the person (me, in your seat, until round 1).
- Builds through autopilot.
- Two blind code-only reads at merge, with criteria logged before reading.
- Builders asked afterwards (now the product's leftover step).
- Periodicals as the independent film.
- A fresh-reader check (the "parrot") on every brief carrying your words.
- Rereading the papers at each milestone.

## Analysis

### 1. The result has three sizes

| Scale | Result | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| A finished move | Better, every time it was finished | Five of five tidy-afters (5 Nov); nine areas over fifteen tickets (paper v2); five of six areas (round 1); Periodicals said "better, small" (5–6 Nov) |
| An ordinary ticket | Not shown | Blind scorers rated the loop's tickets no better than the baseline's (+0.37 against +0.38 and +0.50). New features read "one owner at the centre, a copy at the edge" |
| The whole codebase | Not settling | Stability Review, 4 Nov ("not yet settled") and 8 Nov ("not converging yet") |

The loop cleans what it finishes. It doesn't yet stop new mess on ordinary work (dial 1), and nothing yet drains the debt it already knows about.

Two cautions belong beside that table:
- **The blind-read prompt rewards consolidation**, so it's partly circular.
- **Round 1 has no coherence reading of the whole codebase.** Drift & Coherence and Seams & Tangles were both due and weren't run. The Stability Review measures pace and size, not mess:
  - landings went from 17 to 26 a day;
  - net source grows 1.56k lines a day;
  - CI is up to an 8.55-minute median;
  - evidence files are 55% of repository bytes.

  Much of that is our own programme's activity, and it calls round 1's jump in prompt changes "a burst, not a standing rate". But its core point stands: the project adds far more than it removes (0.35 lines deleted per line added).

### 2. Structure worked; wording didn't

Every mechanism that held up live was structure:
- the sealed ask, which refused my hand-written block;
- cuts named at scoping;
- the fixed size record;
- ratchet censuses that can only fall;
- a sweep defined by its check, not by a grep;
- Periodicals forbidden by a test from filing tickets;
- autopilot's order written once.

Every failure was something unbounded or unstated:
- a close-out crawl with no budget, which caused the outage;
- an area note that hardened into a rule;
- a review told in words to judge against the ask;
- my "how" in a brief, which became the setting.

The measured case is the clearest. Wording did not make review catch a cut nobody had named. Naming the cut upstream did.

### 3. The hand was the main source of error, and it still does essential work

All of these came from this session:

- my generous tally;
- the hand-steering.

That bears out your reading: "the spiral came from this session (the only non-regimented session in the loop…). That's Harbour's whole value."

The hand also still does work Harbour doesn't:

Round 1 moved about six jobs, and about as many remain. In your forcefield picture, the hand is moving from holding the shape to shaping it, and isn't there yet.

### 4. Harbour carried more by growing

- **The footprint:** round 1 added about 4,200 net production lines to Harbour in about 31 hours (+6,709 / −2,500), plus about 9,800 lines of tests and 224,000 lines of review evidence. Harbour is now about 207,000 lines of JavaScript.
- **The cautions it ran into:** the thread's own warnings apply to Harbour's own machinery:
  - build no new nouns;

  - rules get added six times as often as they're removed.
- **The parked fix:** the one ticket aimed at Harbour's own prompts (LIN-4350, fold rules together) was parked.

"Harbour's goal is simple code" and "including our own" are the yardstick this round didn't move.

### 5. Convergence has two halves, and only one is built

  - **Close-out tickets are by design.** Harbour's own rule says close-out files a follow-up for any leftover outside its area. LIN-4388, 4389, 4390 and 4392 came from that rule. My "notes, not tickets" was an operator rule that contradicted it.
  - **About 22 tickets from the thread are still open:** 14 from 4–6 Nov and 8 from round 1.
- **Draining what's known: missing.**

  - Your hypothesis for moving the whole codebase was larger standalone tidies on the Periodicals' worst areas ("Next 1", 6 Nov). It never ran; round 1 came instead.

- **You were asked too often, and wrongly.**

- **Your calls are three:** the size of the move, rulings, and granting authority. On 4 Nov you described the size call as per task: "a task is chosen, surveyed, and the user decides how much to tidy up." The setting turned that into a global switch nobody could read.

## My view: the long term

1. **Make dial 1 a product promise.** "A small-budget ticket goes straight through without making more mess" is the claim that matters on ordinary work, and today it isn't true. It needs the cleanliness judgment inside Harbour:
   - a code-only blind read of the PR before merge (the "after inside the lineage" you parked on 5 Nov);
   - its findings feeding the leftover step, which already fixes in-area edges on the PR.

   That turns "cleaner" from a research measure into the north star's "verified beats claimed".
2. **Build the drain.** Periodicals → area notes works; nothing empties the notes. Two pieces:
   - an idle-time rank that takes one Waiting entry as a small tidy (pulled, bounded);
   - your "Next 1" as a person-sized choice for the worst areas.

   Measure Waiting entries over time and tickets minted per landing (1.43 today). That's what would bend the Stability Review's trajectory.
3. **Point the loop at Harbour.** After the real tasks, give Harbour a subtraction phase:
   - fold the rules (LIN-4350);
   - retire research scaffolding the product now replaces (two Survey voices → one look for small moves; the after-questions → the leftover step);
   - decide evidence in git;
   - take on the growth points the Stability Review names (the test suite's CI time, `dispatch-store.js`).

   One standing rule: every new formal feature names what it retires.
4. **Test generality.** Everything so far ran on Harbour and simple-dispatcher. Two tests are still open:
   - your 4 Nov steer ("as if in a repo where [Periodicals] can't be assumed") and the 5 Nov note: run the loop on a codebase that isn't Harbour;
   - the planted-duplication control, the sharpest version.
5. **Put it on the ladder.** The north star meets people at the saved prompt and walks them up a step at a time. The loop lives only at the top rung, a standing loop. Its parts belong lower:
   - "why is this difficult?" and the priced menu where someone hands over one task;

6. **Cost, once the shape holds.** Round 1 cost about $24 a ticket at API rates. The fixed floor is $5–7 and the Survey pair about $2. Three levers:
   - model defaults (Luna for the free tier, cheaper models for routine stages);

7. **Roles.** You shape: intent, the size of each move, authority and taste. Harbour holds: the loop, the codebase as a place, the film and the drain. This session gets thinner each round, and the aim is that it isn't needed to run a task at all.
