## The question

Can a task finish with the codebase cleaner than when it started? John's tipping point: if tasks, on average, leave the place even a little better, we're in a strong place. Larger improvements that aren't tied to a ticket can stay with the Periodicals.

## The loop as it runs now

1. **Two Survey voices before any work**, both read-only, both allowed to run tests:
   - **open-0 (the task):** "why will this be difficult, and what, if anything, would you do first?" (`prompt.md` / scratchpad `tail/open0-template.md`)
   - **global (the place):** where it lands and how that part is doing; what the obvious way would add, move, tangle, leave homeless or leave in two places; who else must learn about it; what would leave it better at this task's cost; and what is better still but not this task's. Since last night it runs **on the code alone**, with no reviews, reports or maps (`global-offshoot-prompt-v2-code.md`).
2. **When the voices disagree:**
   - on a fact, check the fact (a minute's read);
   - on what to do, run a step-back that lays out the options with prices;
   - when they converge, skip it.
3. **The human's scope call** (Claude in that role, erring larger):
   - each larger extra is its own piece, with "stop and say so if it doesn't pay";
   - when a voice says "keep this separate", honour it;
   - every scope ends with an **at-merge list**, including "who else must learn about it".
4. **The build**, through autopilot.
5. **At merge:**
   - a place reading (`experiments/place-change/place-change.mjs`, with the merge's own `areas.json`);
   - the builder's after-questions (q1–q7: difficulty, what first, what to tidy, the ideal ticket, what the plan got wrong, **what it left behind**, **did the larger scope pay**);
   - a ledger row.
6. **The Periodicals** (Seams, Drift) as the independent film, run from time to time, not routed into tickets (since last night).

## What we've learned

1. **The place view sees what the task view misses, without needing the codebase's reports.** Code-only, every criterion logged before a Survey was hit, except one homeless-file criterion at merge. It worked on simple-dispatcher with no map at all.
2. **The forecast corrects the ticket.**
   - Two of the four ordinary tickets had a stale premise at the base: LIN-4048 was already fixed; LIN-4143's card was already guarded.
   - Two more had a wrong acceptance line.
   - Without the Survey, LIN-4048 would have put back an owner that had been removed on purpose.
3. **Larger scope pays when it's in pieces.**
   - LIN-4143's builder: "the fail-closed card was the only real fix, and it lived in the larger scope."
   - The stop rule worked twice (LIN-4234's piece 3, LIN-4151's renames).
   - The one costly larger scope was where I overrode a voice that said "keep separate".
4. **Pull lands; push sits.**
   - Yesterday, 4 of 4 Periodicals findings routed into in-flight tasks were resolved, and 0 of 17 pushed tickets moved.
   - Overnight we tested without routing: no sign yet that routing was what made the difference.
5. **Builders are the best source of copies, not of the balance.**
   - q6 matched the ledger on 3 of 5 baseline tickets.
   - Builders are blind to the map and kind to their own work.
   - They found copies the place reading missed in four rows overnight.
6. **Ask "who else must learn about it" of the chosen scope, not only of the ticket.** The step-back and the human add things nobody re-checks.
7. **Prose about a joint state lands once, with the last piece** (LIN-4233's comment churn).
8. **The guard for the prompts is generality, not length:** would the sentence make sense on a codebase that isn't Harbour?

2. **The human's scope call** (Claude), written onto the ticket as "How to go about it" plus an at-merge list:
   - **big moves early;** larger extras as their own pieces with stop rules;
   - honour a voice that says "keep separate";
   - **a partial move leaves a ratchet;**
   - don't let a proof-of-no-change rule forbid the small refactor; a byte-identical guard pins wording, not wrapping, and a split path keeps its guarding test (LIN-4277's builder);
   - before writing a witness into the scope, ask whether it can fail at base at all; and where a scope says "the existing tests are the witness", the builder mutation-checks that first, one mutant per behaviour (LIN-4293: two of three views were unpinned);
   - pieces that share a hand-edited list (a census, a registry) run in sequence (LIN-4268/LIN-4269);
   - **a sweep starts by writing its check:** its hit list at the base is the work list, and the check passing is done. Draw the line by behaviour in any spelling, not by a grep or a method list; there's no size exemption from the ratchet rule (LIN-4292's and LIN-4304's builders);
   - test names, not counts: every moved test keeps its name, a test may move to a better-placed suite or join one, the PR maps every name; don't split a file to keep a count (LIN-4296's split-off file copied a fixture);
   - write the scope as instructions, not descriptions: the builder copies a scope's descriptions into comments, and a description that isn't quite true becomes a false comment (LIN-4293's "adds no rule of its own", Drift run 4 row 135);
   - a piece that makes an earlier claim false ("inert", "until X", "nothing calls this yet") retires it in the same change, and code is cited by symbol, not by line (Comprehension-Debt run 5, R1 and R3);
   - check a fact rather than step back over it;
   - a piece that narrows what a test witnesses ships a test for whatever it stops witnessing (LIN-4246's builder);
   - check a blind read's specific claims before filing its tidy-after;
   - a tidy-after that reads better, better gets no tidy-after of its own: its residues go to the notebook, for the next ticket in the area to pull.
3. **During:** a **tidy first, as its own task, where the change binds**, then the follow-on, re-surveyed on the tidied ground.
4. **At merge:** two blind reads; the builder's after-questions q1–q7 once the lineage ends.
5. **After:** the copies a blind read names at the edges become a **small tidy-after, straight away** (Surveyed first, because a blind read's specific claims are claims, not facts).
6. The Periodicals as the film, now and then.
